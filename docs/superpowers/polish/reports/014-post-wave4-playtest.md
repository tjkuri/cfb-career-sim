## The headline metric — the three "do I want to play again" lines

Recorded before starting the next career each time, verbatim:

> **After career 1:** "Yes — I want to play again right now. What's pulling me is the Moments reel
> at the end; it rebuilt my four years into a story I actually recognised, and I want to see what a
> different position's reel looks like. What's pulling against it is that I already know six of the
> cards word-for-word."

> **After career 2:** "Reluctantly yes — but the pull has changed. After career 1 I wanted to play;
> now I want to *check something*, which isn't the same thing. What killed it: the Moments reel
> printed 'Carried the team through bowl season' three times, identical, and that reel was the whole
> reason I came back. And I'd worked out that transferring up every offseason is free OVR, so the
> offseason stopped being a decision."

> **After career 3:** "No. I would stop here. There is nothing left I expect to be surprised by —
> I know the six cards that fire every year, I know the offseason is a menu with one correct answer,
> and I know the ending is a gold headline regardless of what I did. I played the third one because
> you asked."

The trajectory matters more than any single line: **yes → yes-for-the-wrong-reason → no.** The pull
that survived career 1 was the *ending*, not the playing, and career 2 spent it by repeating itself.

## Claims to confirm or refute

### 1. Single-option cards as short narrated beats — **PARTIAL**

They read as *one* event, not two — the description/label/outcome/Continue stack is clean and I never
felt a seam. The format is fine. **The content put in it is often wrong**, in three distinct ways:

- **It asks a question it won't let me answer.** "Extra Film Study — The QB room is open late. Grind
  the tape?" then answers "Study the tape" for me. Same with "Bowl Spotlight — National TV, prime
  time. *Do you rise to it?*" These are the worst ones. Being asked a direct question and having it
  answered on my behalf is more annoying than never being asked.
- **The option label asserts a result the dice then contradict.** "Fall Camp Battle" offers the
  single option "**Win the job**" — and the outcome was "You press, and pressing looks exactly like
  panicking on film. −1 OVR." The label is a verb phrase promising success; I read it as a summary of
  what happened and then had to re-read.
- **When the moment is genuinely inevitable, it works beautifully.** "Senior Day — They call your
  name and you walk out to midfield" → "Soak it in". No choice is *correct* there and it landed as
  the best beat in career 1.

The rule that seems to fall out: a single-option beat works when the option is a posture toward
something unavoidable, and fails when it is phrased as a question or as an achievement.

**Cost as a player:** low individually, but they cluster. Better than a card where I chose? No —
worse, but not much worse, because several two-option cards are also fake (see claim 4 / arbitrary).

### 2. The strip above the card — **PARTIAL, and it is actively lying**

It oriented me on *where* (school, phase, season, class) and I did keep glancing at it — school and
`SEASON n / CLASS` never became furniture, because the school changes when you transfer and that is
the one thing I cared about.

But the strip is the source of the single worst moment in the playtest:

- **The record appears only retroactively.** I finished career 1 season 3 believing I'd had a strong
  year — the cards had told me "comfortable win", "they storm the field", "you put on a show and win
  MVP buzz". Then the offseason strip quietly read **2-10**. I never learned a single result during
  the season. The record isn't hidden so much as it is *revealed too late to mean anything*, and it
  flatly contradicts the narration I'd just been given.
- **The stat line is a season behind during the offseason.** Three offseason cards in a row show last
  season's snaps and stats under this season's number, unlabelled.
- **`1 Game-Winners`** — pluralisation bug, visible in career 2's strip for two whole seasons.

### 3. OVR deltas — **CONFIRMED for magnitude, REFUTED for meaning**

The scaling works, and it works well. `+1 OVR` and `+2 OVR` are small and quiet; `+4 OVR` is
rendered visibly larger and I noticed it without looking for it. That part is solved — don't touch it.

**But I missed several I badly wanted to see, and the ones I got often contradicted the prose.**

- **The biggest single number in the game is never printed at all.** Transferring gives roughly **+5
  OVR, silently** — no card, no text, no delta. I went 64 → 71 and 72 → 79 across two transfers and
  only caught it because I was watching the rail digit. This is a larger swing than any card I ever
  earned, and it is completely invisible. It is also the reason I solved the game by career 2:
  transfer up every offseason, always, and there is never a reason to stay.
- **The number routinely disagrees with the sentence above it.** "Sports media runs a week of
  'availability' takes with your name in the headline" → **+2 OVR**. "It makes a recruiting Twitter
  account. The coordinator does not think it is funny" → **+1 OVR**. "Sprain, not a tear. They shut
  you down early and you keep the rest of the season" (a relieved sentence) → **−5 OVR**, the worst
  hit of that career. After the third of these I stopped reading outcome prose and read only the
  gold number, which is exactly backwards from what this game wants.
- **Emotional stakes and mechanical stakes are uncorrelated.** Shanking a game-deciding onside kick —
  the most vividly written failure in the whole playtest — cost **−1 OVR**, the same as a bad summer.

### 4. The leak check — **mostly NO leak, with one clear exception**

This is largely good news. Across ~105 decisions, resolved cards overwhelmingly describe only what
happened to me. I could not reverse-engineer the unpicked branch, and the same card genuinely gambles:
"Rivalry Week / Play through it" gave me **+2** in one season and **−5** in another. That is a real
gamble and it reads as one.

**The exception, exactly as asked:**

- **"The Ticket Hookup"** → *"Less money, zero risk, and your family gets good seats."* That sentence
  is written as a **comparison to the option I did not take**. It tells me the branch I skipped was
  more money and carried risk. It doesn't tell me whether it would have paid, so it's a
  characterisation leak rather than a value leak — but it's the one card where I finished knowing
  something concrete about the road not taken.
- A softer version: **"Rivalry Week / Sit and get right"** → *"You are healthy for November. It does
  not feel like enough on Saturday."* The second clause is the game telling me I chose the
  emotionally lesser option. It's a nudge, not a number, but it is a thumb on the scale.

Everything else was clean. Do not over-correct — the gamble reads correctly.

### 5. The end-of-career OVR column — **CONFIRMED for long careers, REFUTED for short ones**

Career 1's column is genuinely a story with a shape: **66 / 63 (−3) / 71 (+8) / 83 (+12)** — a
freshman who slipped, found a new school, then leapt. I read that as an arc without being told to,
and the per-season deltas are doing most of that work. Keep them.

Two caveats:

- **Career 3's column is 62 / 63 (+1) — two rows.** A short career produces a stub with no shape at
  all, and the screen doesn't acknowledge that the career was short; it uses the identical layout.
- It reads as a story because the *numbers* happened to arc, not because the design shows an arc. I
  did the shape-finding myself. There is no sparkline, bar, or slope — four right-aligned numerals.
  It works, but it's carried entirely by the deltas.
- **Inconsistency:** career 1's headline says **85 FINAL OVERALL** while the SR row says **83**.
  I noticed immediately and assumed I'd misread something.

### 6. A bad ending printed in dimmed gold — **CANNOT CONFIRM; premise untested, and the real problem is upstream**

**I never reached a bad ending, and I tried.** Career 3 was played deliberately badly — skipped the
install, ghosted the media twice, let my cousin handle the money, played hurt and got wrecked, never
transferred — and declared after **two seasons at OVR 65 with a 5-7 record**. It returned **Round 7,
Pick 2 (#194)**. There appears to be no easily reachable failure state, which means the "gamble"
framing has no visible downside. That is a finding in its own right: I could not lose.

On what I *can* compare — **#69 overall and #194 overall print identically.** Same gold, same size,
same weight, same position on the page. Nothing distinguishes a 3rd-round pick from a near-last pick
at a glance; I had to read the digits. So to the question "can you tell a washout from a draft at a
glance" — I can't even tell a *good* draft from a *bad* one at a glance.

Worse, the surrounding furniture congratulates the bad career anyway: Okonkwo's screen shows an
**HONORS** block reading "Bowl Win" for a player whose two seasons went 7-5 and 5-7, and a Moments
reel led by a trophy emoji. The screen reads as a celebration regardless of input.

### 7. Type smaller and heavier — **CONFIRMED, one specific casualty**

Nothing is shouty; the register feels right and the big OVR numeral and the gold draft headline both
earn their size. One thing is genuinely too small:

- **The third row of the strip — the season stat line.** Exact text, career 2:
  `143 snaps    13 FG 63 Points 1 Game-Winners`. And career 1: `524 snaps    1,193 Pass Yds 8 Pass TD
  3 INT Thrown`. I had to lean toward the screen to read these, every time. It's the only content in
  the game that answers "how am I actually doing", and it's set smaller than anything else on screen.
- Minor: `Seed 1ft6t7` on the start screen and `#69 overall` under the draft headline are both at the
  floor, but neither matters, so I don't care.

### 8. Long text / overflow — **CONFIRMED CLEAN**

Nothing clipped, overflowed, or collided in ~105 cards, including the longest outcome paragraphs
("The Yips", "The Carousel Comes for Your Staff", "Playing Through the Hand") and the longest stat
lines. The card grows to fit and the Continue button follows it down. No horizontal scroll. This one
is genuinely solved.

## The two things nobody had asked about

### Repetition — **worse than the measurement says, and its real cost is indirect**

The brief says five events fire every season and make up ~40% of a career. **My count is higher.**
Guaranteed-feeling recurring cards across my three careers: *Redshirt?*, *Transfer Portal*, *Declare
for the Draft?*, *Fall Camp Battle*, *Going Home for the Summer*, *Season Opener*, *Rivalry Week*,
*Bowl Season* — eight, and in career 1 they were roughly half of my 41 decisions.

**The first word-for-word repeat landed at decision 12** in career 1 (*Going Home for the Summer*),
almost exactly as predicted.

**Did it get worse across the three careers? Yes, and steeply — but not in the way I expected.**
Career 1 the repeats were mildly boring. By career 3 I was not reading the card body *at all* on any
card, new or repeated, because the repeats had trained me that the body text is decoration and the
buttons are the content. **That is the actual cost: the repetition doesn't just make the repeated
cards boring, it makes the good new cards invisible.** "The Blindside", "Family Business" and
"Playing Through the Hand" were all new to me in career 3 and I skimmed every one of them.

What I was skimming by career 3, specifically: every card body paragraph; the "You chose: X" line
(100% ignored, all three careers); the entire strip except the school name and the season number; and
every outcome sentence — I read the gold OVR number and clicked.

**Three structural aggravators that are separate from raw repeat count:**

1. **Adjacent cards ask the same question twice in a row.** This happened in every career:
   *Bowl Season* ("treat it seriously or enjoy it?") is immediately followed by *Fifteen Extra
   Practices* ("treat it seriously or rest?") — the same question, twice, back to back, twice in
   career 2. Also *Bowl Season* → *Bowl Trip Curfew*, and *Season Opener* → *Up Forty at Halftime*.
2. **The worst instance contradicts itself.** Career 3: *Rivalry Week* → "The ankle goes on the second
   series and you watch the rest in a boot." The very next card, *Playing Through the Hand*: "Your
   hand is swollen to twice its size after last week... play or sit." Same dilemma, one card later,
   different body part, and it ignores that I'm already in a boot.
3. **Adjacent cards narrate incompatible games.** Career 2: *Season Opener* resolved "It is 17-14 in
   the fourth and the boo birds are warming up", then *Up Forty at Halftime* opened "The guarantee
   game goes exactly the way guarantee games go" — describing the same game as a blowout.

**And the repetition reached the one thing I praised.** Career 2's Moments reel printed
**"Carried the team through bowl season" three times**, identically, in seasons 1, 3 and 4. Career 3
printed "Had the time of his life at a bowl game" twice. The end-of-career reel is the single best
artifact in the game and the reason I started career 2 — and it repeats itself.

### Career length — **I was wrong about it the whole time, and the title screen lies**

What I believed at the time: the start screen says **"Four years to get drafted"**, so I played
career 1 assuming a hard four-season cap. I never knew how many decisions were left within a season
either — phase (`Offseason / Non-Conference / Conference Play / Postseason`) is the only clue and it
doesn't tell you how many cards each phase holds.

**A fifth season does exist.** I found it by accident in career 2: I took the redshirt in season 2,
and after season 4 the game rolled into **SEASON 5** and offered "Declare or return" again. Nothing
told me the redshirt had bought a year; the *Redshirt?* card says "preserve a year of eligibility"
but never says what that cashes out as, and when season 5 arrived the class label **still read "SR"**
rather than marking it as a bonus year. So the game's own headline promise ("four years") is false,
and its most consequential long-term decision pays out in a currency it never shows you.

Related: **"Take the redshirt (sit the season)" does not sit the season.** I kept playing cards
through season 2 — including *Onside Kick*, a game-deciding kick, in a year where I finished with
**9 snaps and 1 FG**. The snap count honoured the redshirt; the card pool didn't.

### The two quietest greys (DECISION 022) — **PARTIALLY REFUTED**

Split answer, and the split is the useful part.

- **Where the two greys sit on lines of different size and opposite meaning, the difference still
  registers at a normal glance.** The rail's bright headline ("one of the best in the conference")
  against its dim sub-line ("just a guy in the building") is unmistakable. The "You chose: X" line
  under a card title is clearly quieter than the outcome paragraph. No problem there.
- **Where the two greys sit on adjacent lines in the strip, they now read as one flat tone.** Strip
  row 2 (`Baptiste #39 · Conference Play`) and row 3 (`143 snaps · 13 FG 63 Points 1 Game-Winners`)
  are separated **by size, not by colour**. I looked at this at normal viewing distance and then
  magnified to check myself, and my honest read is that the tonal step between them is gone — what
  tells them apart is that row 3 is smaller. If the intent was for row 3 to recede tonally, it isn't
  happening.

So: the call holds for the rail and the card, and does not hold for the strip. And in the strip the
tone question is secondary anyway, because row 3 is too small to read comfortably (claim 7).

## Did the three careers feel different? — **Yes, more than I expected, but only in patches**

Position is **not** a costume, and this surprised me:

- **The stat lines are genuinely position-native** and they're the best orientation in the game:
  `1,193 Pass Yds 8 Pass TD 3 INT Thrown` / `13 FG 63 Points 1 Game-Winners` / `31 Pancakes 14 Sacks
  Allowed`. Reading "Pancakes" told me what kind of player I was faster than anything else on screen.
- **Position-specific cards are the best writing in the game.** The kicker's *Onside Kick*, *The
  Yips* and *The Chip Shot*, and the lineman's *The Blindside*, all felt like they were about
  *me*. "The Yips" — missing eleven in a row on an empty field with only a ball boy watching — is the
  best card I read in three careers.

**But the recurring backbone is position-blind and it breaks the illusion.** The generic cards are
written for a skill player and never adapt:

- My **kicker** was offered "**Lobby for one more series**" (kickers don't take series), and his
  Rivalry Week injury read "**The ankle goes on the second series** and you watch the rest in a boot."
- My **kicker** was told "the twos are taking the reps you would have had" in a card about being
  pulled from a blowout.

So the careers differed by roughly the ratio of position cards to backbone cards — which, because the
backbone is ~half of every career, means they felt maybe 40% different and 60% the same run.

## Where I got bored, confused or lost

- **Bored:** career 1 decision ~12 onward (first repeat), and terminally from career 2 decision ~16.
  Career 3 I was clicking, not playing, from about decision 5.
- **Confused:** career 1 decision 3 — strip said 306 pass yards while the rail said "on the scout
  team / just a guy in the building". I assumed I'd misread the screen.
- **Lost:** every season boundary. Career 1 season 1 → 2 and 2 → 3, career 2 all three, career 3 both.
  **Nothing marks a season ending** — no interstitial, no recap, no "here's what your freshman year
  was". The `SEASON n` digit in the strip changes and a record I've never seen appears. The third
  season blurred into the second exactly the way the brief's example predicted.

## Choices that felt arbitrary

1. **Signing Day, decision 1, every career.** Three school names and nothing else — "Commit to UTSA /
   Stephen F. Austin / South Florida". I had no basis whatsoever and picked on vibes. It's the first
   decision in the game and it teaches you that choices are coin-flips. (Career 3 *did* annotate one
   option — "Commit to Ohio — the two-deep is thin" — which was instantly better. The inconsistency
   makes the unannotated ones feel worse.)
2. **"Declare for the Draft?" — the highest-stakes choice in the game, and the most arbitrary.** At
   OVR 64 as a junior I had no idea whether 64 was draftable. Is the scale 0-100? Is 70 a pro? I
   started at 60 and peaked at 68, so 64 felt mediocre, but that's a guess built from one career.
   The game asks me to bet my career on a number it never contextualises.
3. **Transfer Portal** was arbitrary in career 1 and then *stopped being a choice at all* once I
   noticed the silent +5. By career 2 the offseason was three prompts with one correct answer each.

## What was invisible that should have been visible

- The **transfer OVR bonus** (~+5, the largest effect in the game).
- **Game and season results while they're happening** — I learned I went 2-10 after the fact.
- **What OVR means** — no scale, no draft projection, no "you're currently a 5th-round grade".
- **That a fifth season exists**, and that the redshirt is what buys it.
- **Season boundaries.**

## What was prominent that didn't deserve it

- The **rail's second line**. It occupies prime real estate directly under the OVR number and it is
  the most broken element on screen — see below.
- The **"You chose: X" line**. I never read it once in three careers. I know what I chose; I chose it
  one second ago.

## The rail contradicts itself constantly — this looks like a regression

The two-line rail under the OVR number pairs a headline with a sub-line, and **they disagree far more
often than they agree.** In career 1 the sub-line read **"just a guy in the building" for
approximately 35 consecutive decisions**, across three seasons and two schools, while the headline
climbed from "on the scout team" to "in the rotation" to "a week-one starter" to "one of the best in
the conference" to "All-America caliber". The final pairing was:

> **85** · *All-America caliber* / just a guy in the building

Career 3 produced the inverse — **"on the scout team / the staff trusts you"** while the strip showed
502 snaps. I flag this specifically because a recent commit message in the branch log reads *"the rail
describes one moment, not two"*; whatever that was meant to fix, from the player's chair the rail is
still describing two different moments, and one of them is stuck.

## What made me want to play again / stop

**Again:** the Moments reel, unambiguously. It is the best thing in the game — "Got catfished, handled
it like a champ" made me laugh out loud and made me want to go generate a different one. The
position-specific cards second.

**Stop:** solving the offseason (transfer always, no reason to stay), the backbone repeats training me
to skim, and realising in career 3 that a deliberately bad career still gets a gold headline. Once I
knew I couldn't lose and couldn't be surprised, there was nothing to come back for.

## Smaller things noticed

- Career 1 decision 2, freshman year: "Stay on campus and train" → *"You do exactly what you did last
  summer, and in August it shows that you did."* I had no last summer; it was my first. The text also
  reads like the "go home" branch.
- *The Carousel Comes for Your Staff* fired for my graduating senior, offering "get on the phone
  yourself" — neither option applies to a player who is leaving.
- *The Watch List* is a preseason card; it fired during Non-Conference.
- Career 2 ended with no HONORS block at all despite OVR 91 and a rail that said "All-America
  caliber" for a full season.
- *Speeding Ticket in Georgia* fired for a Tulane player from Louisiana.
- Career 1 season 3 Moments: "Carried the team through bowl season" — that season was 2-10.
- *Signing Day*, *Redshirt?*, *Transfer Portal* and *Declare for the Draft?* all resolve with **no
  outcome card at all** — they jump straight to the next card. Three of them fire consecutively in
  the offseason, which makes the offseason feel like a settings menu rather than part of the story.

## If I could change three things

1. **Mark the end of a season.** One screen: record, stat line, OVR change, one sentence. It would fix
   the boundary problem, the retroactive-record problem, and give the repeats a frame that makes them
   read as *annual rituals* rather than as bugs.
2. **Print the transfer delta, and contextualise OVR.** Show the +5. Show what the number means
   ("currently projected: 6th round"). Both the game's biggest lever and its biggest decision are
   currently blind.
3. **Make the outcome prose and the OVR number agree**, and stop asking questions in single-option
   cards. These two together are what taught me to stop reading, and the writing is much too good for
   that to be the outcome.
