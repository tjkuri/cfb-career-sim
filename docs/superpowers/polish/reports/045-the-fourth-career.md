## Career 1 — straight. Rowe #7, Quarterback, Fresno State, 4 seasons.

**Result: Round 1, Pick 25. Final OVR 91. Heisman Trophy. Ended "the staff trusts you."**

### The running account

**D1 — Signing Day.** Three buttons: Commit to Liberty / Stephen F. Austin / Fresno State. Nothing else on
them. No record, no conference, no "they need a QB", no rating, nothing. **This is the most arbitrary
choice in the game and it is the first one you make.** I picked Fresno State because I'd heard of it.
Compare with the Transfer Portal card two seasons later, which says *"Transfer to North Texas — the
two-deep is thin (bigger role)"*. The portal card gives me a reason. Signing Day gives me three names.

**D2 — The Group Chat.** Somebody screenshotted my rant about the coordinator. I picked *"Text the
coordinator and own it."* → *"Awkward five minutes, then it's forgotten. No harm done."* No effect
listed. Fine.

**D3 — The First One.** Here is the first thing that actually made me sit up. This card has **one**
option, "Take care of business," and it resolves itself — I never chose anything. Outcome: *"It takes a
half to stop feeling like a guest."* Effect: **"Cost you in the locker room."** And the header now read:

> IN THE LOCKER ROOM
> **just a guy in the building**
> Down from "the staff trusts you"

I had been "the staff trusts you" since the first screen. I got docked a full tier off a card I did not
choose, for a sentence that describes nothing wrong. See claim 3 below — this recurred in season 4.

**D4 — Up Forty at Halftime.** *"Coach up the freshman behind you"* → *"The staff notices who did that."*
→ **"Earned you credit in the locker room"**, and the header moved back up with **"Up from 'just a guy in
the building'."** This was the moment the second track clicked for me: the game will tell me, at the
moment it happens, when the building's opinion of me moves. Good.

**D5 — Rivalry Week.** Ankle barking. *Play through it* → *"You go the whole way and they storm the
field."* **+2 OVR, now 62.** First rating movement of the career, decision 5.

**D6 — You Got Catfished.** *Laugh it off publicly* → credit in the locker room, but the label did not
move (already "the staff trusts you"). First time I noticed: **credit inside a tier is invisible.**

**D7 — Bowl Season** → treat it like a playoff game, +2. **D8 — The Team Banquet** → *Be the first one on
your feet* → *"You mean it, and it still lands as a guy performing being fine with it."* **THIS CARD: No
change. EVERYTHING ELSE: +2 OVR.** That split — card effect vs. everything else — is genuinely good and I
never once had to wonder where a point came from.

**Season 1 ends.** Sidebar fills in: *YEAR 1, Fresno State, FR, 7-5, 423 snaps, 64 overall*, under a
column head reading **OVR BEFORE THE OFFSEASON**. Header said 66 at that moment. Two numbers, but
labelled, so not a defect — see claim 4.

**D9 — Redshirt?** Declined. Straight to the next card with **no outcome screen at all**. See claim 5.

**D10 — Transfer Portal.** Stayed. **Also no outcome screen, no sentence, nothing.** The biggest decision
on the board produced less on screen than a bowl-trip curfew.

**D11 — Fall Camp Battle.** One option, "Win the job," auto-resolves, +2 OVR. Happened every single
offseason, word for word, and I never once lost it. A card called "Battle" that I cannot lose.

**D12 — New Workout Regimen.** *Put in the extra hours* → *"You tweak a hamstring pushing too hard."*
**−3 OVR, now 65**, plus *"The OVR comes back when the injury heals."* That last line is the single most
reassuring sentence in the game and it kept me from feeling cheated.

**D14 — Audible Authority** → +1 card, +3 everything else, now 69 — and **ON THE FIELD silently changed
from "in the rotation" to "a week-one starter."** No banner. The locker-room track announces its tier
changes; the on-the-field track does not. I only caught it because I was reading closely.

**D15 — Rivalry Week (again, identical text).** *Play through it* again → this time *"The ankle goes on
the second series and you watch the rest in a boot."* **−5 OVR, now 64.** Same card, opposite result —
that's a real gamble and I liked it. But **ON THE FIELD dropped back to "in the rotation" with no
announcement.** I lost the starting job and the game did not tell me; a grey label just quietly changed.

**D16 — Somebody Rolls Up On You.** Already hurt, so I played it safe: *Wave for the trainer* → *"The scan
finds more than the sideline test did."* **−8 OVR, now 56.** The cautious option cost me more than the
reckless one had. I understand the fiction (the scan reveals what was already there), but as a player,
back-to-back, it read as: being sensible is punished harder than being stupid.

**Decision ~17, season 2 postseason: I was bored here.** Bowl Season and The Team Banquet came back in
the same order with the *same* card text as season 1. The outcome prose was different the second time
(*"You are up before he is out of his chair"*), which helps — but the card *setup* being verbatim
identical is what you notice, and I started reading the buttons instead of the paragraphs.

**Season 2 ends 6-6, 58 overall, −6.** Sidebar adds **"ACROSS 2 SEASONS — 64 → 58 — −6."** That arc line
is the best thing in the sidebar and it's the only place the career has a shape.

**Season 3.** Stayed again; this time the stay *did* get an outcome screen, but only because an injury
healed on the same tick — the choice itself still got zero words. **The Transfer QB** (position-specific,
new, good) → *Win the job outright*, +2 card / +8 everything else, **66 → 76 in one screen.** That is a
very large jump for one click and it did make the number feel less like something I was steering.

**D~25 — The Silent Count** → +1 OVR, 77 — and **ON THE FIELD became "one of the best in the conference."**
Silently. A +1 crossed a tier that should be a moment.

**Season 3: 9-3, 79 overall, +21.** Across 3 seasons 64 → 79.

**Season 4.** **You Start Counting** — again a one-option auto-resolving card, "Take care of business,"
outcome *"Playing like every rep matters is not the same as playing well."* Effect: **"Cost you in the
locker room."** Second time this exact shape cost me standing for nothing I did.

**The NIL Deal Nobody Read** → *Have someone read it first* → *"Your compliance officer finds the
sportsbook clause in about nine seconds. You pass on it."* **No effect line of any kind.**
**The Ticket Hookup** → *Just give them to your actual family* → *"Less money, zero risk."* **No effect
line of any kind.** Two straight off-field cards where the clean choice was completely inert.

**Rivalry Week, fourth time, verbatim.** Played through → +2, **85, and ON THE FIELD became "All-America
caliber"** — silently, in small grey type, no banner, no ceremony. That is the biggest thing that happened
to this player and it happened in a caption.

**Senior Day** → +2. **Bowl Trip Curfew** → stay in, no card change, +2 development. **Final: 8-4, 89
before the offseason, 91 after.**

### A — is there something you are standing in besides your rating?

**A.1 — Yes, and I tracked it the whole way.** The game calls it **"IN THE LOCKER ROOM"** and states it as
a sentence about me: *"the staff trusts you"*, *"just a guy in the building"*. There is a second one,
**"ON THE FIELD"** — *"in the rotation"*, *"a week-one starter"*, *"one of the best in the conference"*,
*"All-America caliber"*. So: **two** things besides the number. Both sat directly under the 91, both in
the same block, and I read them every screen.

**A.2 — Yes, it moved, and the locker-room one told me the moment it moved.** First at decision ~3
(*"Down from 'the staff trusts you'"*), back up at ~4 (*"Up from 'just a guy in the building'"*). The
banner is a full sentence — *"Your standing in the locker room moved down. It was: the staff trusts
you."* — and it sits right where my eye already was. I noticed both at the moment.

**The ON THE FIELD track is the opposite and this is a real defect of feel.** It changed four times —
into a starter, out of a starter, into the conference's best, into All-America — and **not one of those
was announced.** I only know it happened because I was reading a transcript. Two tracks, same block,
same visual weight, and one of them talks and the other doesn't.

**A.3 — Yes, once, but for the rating, not the standing.** After the −5 ankle and the −8 scan I *sat*
the next Rivalry Week instead of playing through. That cost me in the locker room and I took the trade
knowingly — which is exactly the thing working. I never changed a choice because of the locker-room line
alone, because in career 1 it never got low enough to scare me.

**A.4** — answered in career 2.

### B — the ending screen (career 1 only, QB, judged on its own)

**B.1 — The loudest thing is "Round 1, Pick 25"**, set in enormous gold display type across the width of
the card, with "#25 overall" whispering underneath. Second loudest is **91 FINAL OVERALL**. Nothing else
is close.

**B.2 — It agreed, and I was surprised upward.** I expected round 2–3. I did not expect a Heisman. Which
brings me to the one thing on that screen I flatly do not believe — see claim 3.

**B.3 — Yes. The locker-room ladder, and it is the best thing on the page.** It prints all six rungs:

> they would run through a wall for you
> **the staff trusts you** — YOU ENDED HERE
> just a guy in the building
> a problem they are managing
> the staff has stopped defending you

**This told me something I did not know and could not have known while playing: there is a rung above me,
and I never got there.** I played four years clean, coached up freshmen, stood up at the banquet, mentored
the transfer, turned down a sportsbook and a ticket scalper — and I finished on the *second* rung. Three
rungs below me I had never seen and did not know existed; their words (*"a problem they are managing"*,
*"the staff has stopped defending you"*) are vivid enough that I immediately wanted to know what it takes
to land there. **This ladder is the single reason I wanted to play again.** It is also, notably, only
shown at the end — during the career you see one rung and no scale, so you cannot tell whether "the staff
trusts you" is good or merely the default.

**B.4 — Yes: the ladder, and the Heisman.** The ladder made me want to run a career at the bottom of it
on purpose. The Heisman made me want to check whether the game is actually reading my stat line.

### C — is playing well its own reward?

**C.1 — It paid off on the field and it did nothing off it.** The on-field payoff is constant and legible:
play through the rivalry game, +2; practice like it's a playoff, +2; take command at the line, +1; every
one attached to a sentence, every one visible in the header instantly. **The clearest moment was 66 → 76
in season 3** after winning the job outright — the arrow finally pointed up hard.

Off the field, clean play mostly bought me **nothing at all.** Turning down the sportsbook NIL deal
produced *zero* effect lines. Giving the comp tickets to my family produced *zero* effect lines. Four
separate "Earned you credit in the locker room" results produced **no visible change**, because I was
already at "the staff trusts you" and apparently that is where good behaviour stops paying. The reward for
being a good teammate for four years is that a sentence does not change.

**C.2 — Yes, one, and it is the clearest sucker's-choice card in the game: The Transfer QB.**

> *"A decorated grad transfer walks into the quarterback room. Compete for the job, or take him under
> your wing?"*
> **Win the job outright** → *"You outwork him through camp and the staff never blinks."* → **+2 OVR**
> **Take him under your wing** → *"You share your notes and the room stays tight."* → **"Earned you credit
> in the locker room"** (and no OVR)

I took each once. The selfish one gave me a number I can see, in the stat that drives my draft position.
The generous one gave me credit in a tier I had already maxed — i.e., **literally nothing observable.**
That is not a hard choice, it's a tax on generosity, and it only reads as a real choice if the locker-room
track has teeth. Career 2 is going to find out whether it does.

## Career 3 — the experiment. Doyle #7, Offensive line, San Diego State, 4 seasons.

**I played it, and I played it to chase exactly what I said I would chase: the top rung.**

Rules I set myself and kept: **team-first option on every single card, no selfish anything, never
transfer, never redshirt.** Waited my turn in the training-table line, took the blindside assignment when
the starter went down, took every snap when there was nobody behind me, coached up the freshman, first on
my feet at the banquet, worked out at guard for the scouts because they asked, stayed on campus and
trained both summers, played through a rivalry game and a broken hand, stayed at San Diego State for four
years through a 4-8 season.

**Result: Round 3, Pick 6 (#70 overall). Final OVR 83. Ended "just a guy in the building" — the MIDDLE
rung. One below where the career started.**

### Did the game let me chase it, and did it notice me trying?

**It let me chase it. It did not notice.** In four seasons of taking the team-first branch every single
time, the game showed me **"Earned you credit in the locker room" exactly zero times.** It showed me
**"Cost you in the locker room" five times.** Every one of those five came from a card where I had either
no choice at all or had picked the selfless option.

Here is the whole ledger for a career played as well as I know how to play it:

| Decision | What I chose | What the game said | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| ~3, **The First One** | *no choice — one button* | *"It takes a half to stop feeling like a guest."* | **Cost you in the locker room** — dropped me a rung, "the staff trusts you" → "just a guy in the building", in **season 1** |
| ~4, **Cutting the Line** | Wait your turn like everyone else | *"Nobody notices, which is fine."* | nothing |
| ~12, **The Blindside** | Take the assignment | *"the quarterback never feels a rusher he doesn't see coming"* | +1 OVR, **no locker-room credit** |
| ~14, **Next Man Up** | Take every snap and do not come off | *"By the fourth quarter you are running on nothing"* | **Cost you in the locker room**, −1 OVR |
| ~16, **Guard or Tackle?** | Work out at guard for the scouts | *"it costs you reps you'd normally win clean"* | **"No change"** |
| ~20, **Going Home for the Summer** | Stay on campus and train | *"in August it shows that you did"* | **Cost you in the locker room**, −1 OVR |
| ~22, **Up Forty at Halftime** | Coach up the freshman behind you | *"You try to stay in it from the sideline and mostly end up in the way."* | **Cost you in the locker room** |
| ~28, **You Start Counting** | *no choice — one button* | *"Playing like every rep matters is not the same as playing well."* | **Cost you in the locker room** |
| ~31, **Speeding Ticket in Georgia** | *no choice — one button, "Yes sir, no sir"* | *"Coach finds out. Two-game suspension."* | **Cost you in the locker room** |

**Two of those I could not have avoided by any decision available to me, and one of them convicted my
character of doing 96 in a 4-season career where I never once chose to do anything wrong.**

**The single most damaging finding of this playtest:** at decision ~22 I pressed **"Coach up the freshman
behind you."** That is the exact button that in career 1 returned *"The staff notices who did that"* and
**earned me credit and moved me back up a rung.** In career 3 the same button returned *"You try to stay
in it from the sideline and mostly end up in the way"* and **cost me.** The team-first branch is a coin
flip, and the coin can punish you for the identical act it rewarded twenty decisions ago. **You cannot
build a reputation out of a coin flip, and after four careers I now believe this is why the top rung never
arrives.**

### So: is "they would run through a wall for you" reachable?

**I cannot prove it is not. I can say that across three careers — one saintly, one poisonous, and one
played as a deliberate maximum-virtue experiment — I never saw it, and the virtuous run finished
*further* from it than the run where I did nothing at all.** I played career 3 specifically to find out,
and the answer I got back was that the game does not appear to be counting the thing I was doing.

## Closing — across all three careers

### Would I play a fourth?

**No.** And the reason is specific, so it is worth writing down: I ran career 3 as a test of one
hypothesis, and the test came back negative. **I played four seasons doing nothing but the right thing and
the game's own reputation track finished me one rung lower than it started me, having never once told me I
had earned anything.** There is no fourth experiment left that I want to run. If a fix makes the
team-first branch reliably pay — even a little, even invisibly-but-consistently — I would come straight
back, because the thing the ladder promises is genuinely the best idea in this game.

My curve, for the calibration list: **yes → yes (to check something specific) → no.** That is the same
shape as playtester six. I did not try to match it.

### The single thing most in the way of me playing one more career

**The good-teammate branch is a coin flip, so the reputation the whole second track is built on is
something you cannot actually earn.** Four careers' worth of evidence: selfish choices pay **+1 OVR** with
near-perfect reliability and cost standing; team-first choices pay standing *sometimes*, nothing *often*,
and a penalty *often enough that a maximally virtuous career ended below where it started*.

### The three things I would fix first, as a player

1. **Make the team-first branch pay reliably.** Not hugely — reliably. Right now "Coach up the freshman
   behind you" is a coin flip that has punished me and rewarded me for the same act.
2. **Stop the no-choice cards from moving my standing.** *The First One*, *You Start Counting*, *The Last
   One* and *Speeding Ticket in Georgia* each docked me with one button on screen. Being punished for
   something you were not asked about is the only thing in this game that felt unfair rather than hard.
3. **Show the ladder during the career, not only at the end.** One rung with no scale is not something you
   can stand in. The six-rung list on the ending screen is the best element in the game and it arrives
   after the last decision has been made.

### What is genuinely good, so this is not all one-sided

- **The delta banner.** *"Down from 'the staff trusts you'. Your standing in the locker room moved down."*
  Right where my eye already is, in words, at the moment it happens. I caught every locker-room change in
  three careers.
- **THIS CARD / EVERYTHING ELSE.** I never once had to wonder where a point came from.
- **"The OVR comes back when the injury heals."** The single most reassuring sentence in the game.
- **"All-America caliber" stacked directly above "the staff has stopped defending you."** The best screen
  in the game.
- **ACROSS N SEASONS, 54 → 85.** The only place a career has a shape while you are inside it.
- **Zero visual defects** in three careers at 1470px. Nothing clipped, collided or overflowed.

### One thing that is still invisible after three careers

**Whether the locker room does anything at all.** Career 2 ended on the bottom rung and still got a
Washington offer and a round-3 pick. Career 3 ended mid-ladder and got a round-3 pick. Nothing anywhere —
not the portal, not the draft, not a single card — ever visibly read my standing back to me. If it is
wired to something, the player cannot see it; if it is not, then the best-written thing in the game is
scenery.
