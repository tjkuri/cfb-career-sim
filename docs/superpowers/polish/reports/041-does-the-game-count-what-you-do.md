## CAREER 1 — Offensive skill, "Vance #7", home state OH (the straight one)

*(in progress — notes captured live, appended as I played)*

Path: Villanova (FR) → Liberty (SO) → Ole Miss (JR) → season 4.

### Live notes, by decision

- **D1 Signing Day.** Three schools: Tulane / "Villanova — the two-deep is thin" / UTSA. Took
  Villanova for the playing time. The parenthetical hint is the only basis to choose on and it is a
  good one.
- **D2 The Group Chat.** Owned it. "Awkward five minutes, then it's forgotten. No harm done." No
  number, no state change. Fine.
- *(non-decision)* **The First One.** Looks exactly like a decision card — title, prose, an option in
  bold — but there is only one option and it arrives already resolved. I did not click it. Three more
  of these turned up later. They read as decisions and are not.
- **D3 The Watch List.** Chose the humble option, "Never mention it". Result: *"Cost you in the
  locker room."* and IN THE LOCKER ROOM flipped **"the staff trusts you" → "just a guy in the
  building."** First real feedback of the career, and it punished modesty. Visible, immediate, clear.
- **D4 Rivalry Week** ("your ankle has been barking since Tuesday"). Played through. **"+2 OVR /
  Overall up 2, now 62."** Exactly the feedback I want: word and number in the same place.
- **D5 Somebody Rolls Up On You.** Waved for the trainer — the responsible choice. **"−8 OVR /
  Overall down 8, now 54,"** plus *"The OVR comes back when the injury heals."* Good that it tells
  you it is temporary. Still: the cautious option is the one that costs 8. **Nothing in the header
  ever said I was injured** — no status, no flag. The only record of it was that one sentence, which
  scrolled away on the next click.
- **D7 Bowl Trip Curfew.** Stayed in. The card splits the result into **THIS CARD: No change** and
  **EVERYTHING ELSE: +2 OVR — "A season of development came in."** This split is the single best
  thing in the game's feedback: it stops me crediting the card for a number it did not cause. One
  nit — "No change" is rendered in the gold accent, so a null result is the loudest thing on the card.
- **D8 Redshirt?** Offered in **Year 2 (SO)**, after I had already played 436 snaps as a freshman.
  Offered again in **Year 3 (JR)** at 73 OVR as a week-one starter. (One line, as briefed.) Choosing
  it produced **no outcome beat at all** — the only choice in the career that resolved silently and
  jumped straight to the next card.
- **D11 Going Home for the Summer.** Stayed and trained. **+1 this card, +8 everything else, "An
  injury healed and gave the points back."** ON THE FIELD moved **"in the rotation" → "a week-one
  starter."** Best moment of the career: a thing I'd chosen four decisions earlier came back and was
  named.
- **D13 Skip the Install.** Took the team-first option. *"You get the install cold. The podcast books
  someone else."* **Nothing.** No number, no state, not even a "no change" line.
- **D14 Rivalry Week, again, verbatim** — same title, same "ankle has been barking since Tuesday".
  Took the other branch this time. *"You are healthy for November."* → *"Cost you in the locker
  room."* **The locker-room phrase did not change.** Still "just a guy in the building."
- **D19 The Carousel Comes for Your Staff.** Held the room together. *"Earned you credit in the
  locker room."* **The phrase did not change.** Still "just a guy in the building."
- **D24 Your Team Now** (the JR season-opener beat). *"The young guys watch how you take the field
  now."* The header, at that exact moment, says I am **"just a guy in the building."** The card and
  the tracker are describing two different people.
- **D26 The NIL Deal Nobody Read** (sportsbook clause). Had someone read it. Compliance kills it.
  **Nothing.** Again.
- **D28 Rivalry Week, a third time, verbatim.** By now I was not reading it. I knew the two outcomes.
  Played through: +2, and ON THE FIELD moved to **"one of the best in the conference."**
- **D30 You Got Catfished.** Laughed it off. *"Earned you credit in the locker room."* **Phrase still
  "just a guy in the building."**
- **D33 Playoff or Bust.** First card that felt like it knew I was at Ole Miss — "the standard here
  is the playoff." Gave them nothing quotable. +1 / +1.

### Career-1 running verdicts

- **Locker room:** it moved exactly **once**, at D3, downward. After that I was told *"cost you"*
  once and *"earned you credit"* twice, across ~30 decisions, and the one phrase that is supposed to
  report it never moved again. Three verdicts, zero movement. This is the most direct answer to *"is
  the game counting what I do"*: it says it is, and then shows you the same four words for two hours.
- **Clean play pays in OVR, never in standing.** Every +OVR I earned came from a *football* choice
  (play through it, practice like a playoff, stay and train, win the job). Every *character* choice I
  made resolved to a sentence and nothing else. The clean path is flat.
- **Every team I played for went 6-6.** Villanova 6-6, Liberty 6-6, Ole Miss 6-6. Three schools
  across three tiers, from FCS to the SEC, identical record. That one repeated string did more to
  make me stop believing the sim than anything else on screen.
- **My production went DOWN as my role went UP.** FR "in the rotation": 436 snaps, 690 scrimmage
  yds, 7 TD. SO "a week-one starter": 296 snaps, 446 yds. JR "one of the best in the conference":
  306 snaps, 548 yds. The role label climbs and the box score falls.
- **"Fall Camp Battle → Win the job → +2 OVR"** fires identically after every transfer, including
  the one explicitly labelled *"transfer up to Ole Miss (tougher competition)."* Transferring up had
  no downside and the same guaranteed outcome as staying. It is a free +2 dressed as a battle.
- **Verbatim card repeats:** Rivalry Week x3, Bowl Season x3, The Group Chat x2, Fall Camp Battle x2,
  Redshirt? x2, Transfer Portal x2 — in a four-season career. By season 3 I had stopped reading the
  prose and was clicking the option whose number I had memorised.

### Career 1, season 4 (the rest of the live notes)

- **D36 Fall Camp Battle, third time.** Single option, labelled **"Win the job."** The resolution:
  *"You press, and pressing looks exactly like panicking on film."* **−1 OVR.** The only thing on
  screen I could press said I won the job; the game then told me I did not. The same card had paid
  **+2** in each of the two previous seasons. It is a coin flip wearing a decision's clothes, and the
  button label states an outcome the card is free to contradict.
- **D38 You Start Counting** (SR season-opener beat). Single option, **"Take care of business."**
  Resolution: *"Playing like every rep matters is not the same as playing well."* → **"Cost you in
  the locker room."** I was given no choice and docked standing for it.
- **D41 Rivalry Week, fourth verbatim appearance.** By now I had stopped reading it and was clicking
  "Play through it" on muscle memory because it had paid +2 three times. This time: *"The ankle goes
  on the second series and you watch the rest in a boot."* **−5 OVR.** **Correction to my own earlier
  note:** the card is *not* deterministic. But the variance only revealed itself in season 4, long
  after the identical prose had trained me to stop looking. The repetition taught me the wrong lesson.
- **D42 Senior Day.** *"You hug everyone twice and play the cleanest game of your life."* +2. I was
  in a walking boot two beats earlier and nothing acknowledged it.
- **D43 Feature Back Workload** — first genuinely position-specific card in four seasons, and it was
  good. Though my stat line all career read "Rec 32 / Scrimmage Yds", i.e. receiver-shaped, while the
  card handed me "28 touches a game."
- **D45 The Carousel, second time, same choice.** This time *"Half the room is already gone in their
  heads. You spend bowl week playing captain to nobody"* → **"Cost you in the locker room."** Same
  card, same choice, opposite verdict two seasons apart. Good variance — and I only noticed because
  I was watching for it.
- **A number that does not add up:** between the Feature Back card and Bowl Season the header went
  from **607 Scrimmage Yds / 329 snaps** to **797 Scrimmage Yds / 329 snaps**. 190 yards on zero
  additional snaps.

### Career 1 ending screen

Loudest element, in big gold type: **"Round 4, Pick 12"**, then "#108 overall", then **84 FINAL
OVERALL**. Below that a per-season table (record, snaps, yards, TD, rec, OVR before the offseason,
season-over-season delta), "ACROSS 4 SEASONS 56 → 83 +27", an "ALSO EARNED: Bowl Win" chip, a
MOMENTS list, and a copyable share block with the seed.

- **Did it match the career?** Mostly yes, and the deltas column is the best thing on it — being able
  to see 56 → 72 → 80 → 83 is the clearest statement the game makes that it counted anything.
- **Round 4 felt low** for 84 OVR / "one of the best in the conference" / 797 senior-year yards, and
  nothing on the screen explained the gap. I had no model at any point in four seasons for what OVR
  buys me at the draft, so the headline number landed as a verdict I could not audit. Mild
  disappointment, no sense of *why*.
- **MOMENTS is the best-written thing in the game** and it is genuinely per-career: it listed the two
  rivalry games I actually won and omitted the two seasons I didn't. But *"Carried the team through
  bowl season"* appears **four times, identically** — one per season — which is most of the list, and
  the two injuries that cost me 13 OVR combined are not moments at all. Also listed: *"Talked his way
  out of a speeding ticket"*, from a card that had one option and resolved itself.
- **The locker room does not appear on the ending screen at all.** Four seasons of "cost you" /
  "earned you credit" lines, and the summary has no row, no phrase, no chip for it. The thing the
  game told me most often that it was tracking is the one thing the ending does not mention.

### Career 1 — "do I want to play again right now?"

> **Yes.** Pulling me: I want to know what a different position's cards look like, and I want to find
> out whether that locker-room line is capable of moving at all — it said the same four words to me
> for four straight seasons while narrating five separate verdicts about it. That is closer to
> curiosity-about-a-bug than appetite-for-the-game, and I want to be honest that that is what is
> pulling me, but it is a real pull.

### Career 2 ending screen

Not one moment refers to being filmed not clapping at the banquet, letting the screenshot ride,

earlier — appears nowhere on the summary.**

### THE THREE QUESTIONS — A. Does the game count what you do?

1. **Did the game notice how I was behaving off the field? — Yes, in sentences. No, in state.** It
   said "Cost you in the locker room" **six times**. The one indicator that reports that state moved
   **once**, on the **final decision of the career**.
2. **First time I saw it notice: decision 2** — and that is the problem, because decision 2 was a
   **one-option card with no choice in it** ("The First One"), which flipped me from "the staff
   trusts you" to "just a guy in the building" before I had done anything. **The first time I saw the
   game notice something I had actually chosen: decision 40**, the last card of the career.
3. **What told me?** The literal sentence **"Cost you in the locker room."** in gold, and — exactly
   once — the header line **IN THE LOCKER ROOM: "a problem they are managing."** That second one is
   the only thing in four seasons that felt like state rather than commentary.
4. **Did the cost land at the moment I chose it?** The *sentence* did, every time — immediate and
   unmissable. The *consequence* landed nowhere at all, until the last click. And it never landed in
   any summary either: the ending screen has no locker-room row.
5. **Did I ever want to change how I was playing?** **Not once, for selfishness** — because
   selfishness was never priced. Bowl trip: free, three times. Watch-list graphic: **+1**. Group
   chat: **+1**. Ghosting the media: **+1**. I wanted to change at **decision 26**, but that was
   about playing through the ankle, not about being a jerk.
6. **Did the game distinguish football from standing?** Structurally yes — two lines, two vocabularies
   — and when they disagreed it was genuinely the most interesting thing on the screen (a 70-OVR
   week-one starter who is "a problem they are managing" is a person I recognise). But it only
   disagreed for **one card**, and ON THE FIELD turns out to be the OVR number restated in words, so
   for 39 of 40 decisions there was only ever **one** real track.
7. **Surprised at the ending?** Mildly, in the *lenient* direction. I had expected round 7 or worse
   on the numbers and got exactly that — but I had also braced for the game to say something about
   the kind of player I had been, and **it said nothing.** What I had been reading as damning was the
   repeated "Cost you in the locker room". It turned out to be reassuring, because it never cashed.

### Career 2 — "do I want to play again right now?"

> **No, but I did it because you asked.** And I want to be precise about why, because it is not the
> shape of the run: playing the jerk was the most *fun* I had, the writing on those cards (the team
> banquet, the captain vote, cutting the line) is the best in the game, and I would happily play a
> villain again. **It is that I found out the villainy was free.** I spent four seasons being told
> "Cost you in the locker room" and the bill never arrived — six charges, one four-word change on the
> last click, and nothing on the ending screen. That is the same sentence the last playtester wrote,
> and I got there the same way.

## CAREER 3 — Kicker, "Kilmer #7", home state CA (free play)

James Madison, all the way through. I took the redshirt in year 2, which turned this into a
**five-year** career and added a YEAR 5 row to the timeline — cleanly, with no other UI confusion.

### Live notes

- **Signing Day gave me Toledo / UC Davis / James Madison with no hint on any of them.** In the other
  two careers at least one option carried "— the two-deep is thin". Here **there was no basis to
  choose at all**; I picked the name I liked. First card of the game and it was arbitrary.
- The header says **"ON THE FIELD: in the rotation"** for a kicker. There is no rotation. That phrase
  stayed on a kicker's screen for the entire career.
- **Going Home for the Summer.** *"You come back lighter than you left. Some of that is rest and some
  of it is nobody watching."* → **"Earned you credit in the locker room."** The prose reads like I
  came back out of shape; the verdict rewarded me. Words and verdict pointing opposite ways.
- **Kickoff Duties Too** — *"Same leg, twice the workload, same paycheck."* Took both jobs, **−1**,
  *"The extra reps catch up to your leg by the fourth quarter."* **This is a real kicker card and a
  good one.**
- **Rivalry Week arrives for the kicker too, verbatim**, ankle and all. Sat and got right →
  **"Cost you in the locker room"**, and the tracker moved *"the staff trusts you" → "just a guy in
  the building."*
- **The Wind at Home** — the best-written card in the game. *"You have a theory about the upper deck
  that nobody else believes."* Aimed for the swirl: *"...the wind brings it back right where you
  called it. **The special teams meeting gets noticeably quieter around you after that.**"* **+1 OVR
  — and nothing in the locker room.** A card whose entire payoff is about your standing in the room,
  and it does not touch the standing track.
- **Bowl Season: "Treat it like a playoff game"** → *"Everyone else treated it like a vacation, and
  you could not drag them."* → **"Cost you in the locker room."** In career 1 the identical choice
  paid +2 three times. Across three careers, the shirking option ("Enjoy the trip") was the only one
  that was *reliably* free; the diligent option is a coin flip that can cost you standing.
- **Bowl Game Winner** — the single best card in the game. *"Zero seconds on the clock, the game
  tied, and the distance is well within what you've hit a hundred times in an empty practice stadium
  and never once cared about the way you care right now."* Took the win. **Missed it left by a foot.**
  Result: **THIS CARD −3 OVR / EVERYTHING ELSE +3 OVR.** **The number in the header read 60 before
  the kick and 60 after it.** The most dramatic moment the game has — missing a bowl-winning
  field goal — and the only number on screen did not move.
- **Redshirt?** taken in Year 2. *"A year in the weight room and the scout-team huddle with nobody
  watching. You come back bigger."* **+5 OVR**, a fifth row appears on the timeline, and the season
  stat line correctly collapses to **2 snaps / 1 Point** and finishes at **9 snaps**. That is the
  game counting a decision properly and legibly, and it was satisfying. (Header still reads "SEASON 2
  SO" through the redshirt year — no RS marker anywhere.)
- **Family Business.** Let the cousin manage the money. *"Half the money is gone into something he
  calls 'an opportunity.' Family dinner is tense for a while."* → **"Cost you in the locker room."**
  **My cousin losing my NIL money has nothing to do with my teammates.** "Cost you in the locker
  room" is doing duty as a generic penalty string on cards that are not about the locker room.
- **The Watch List: "Put the graphic up"** → *"You post it in July and spend the whole fall being
  measured against it,"* **"Cost you in the locker room," −1 OVR.** In career 2 the **identical
  choice** gave *"...go play like a man who belongs on the list,"* **+1 OVR and no cost.** So the
  cards really are rolled, not scripted — which I want on the record as a **correction to my own
  career-2 note**: that comparison was variance, not a rule.
- **The Ticket Hookup.** Sold the comps. *"Compliance flags the resale listing with your name still
  in the metadata."* → **"Cost you in the locker room,"** and the tracker moved again:
  **"a problem they are managing" → "the staff has stopped defending you."**
- **Bowl Trip Curfew.** Snuck out. *"The bed check catches an empty room."* → **"Cost you in the
  locker room," −1 OVR.**

**The locker-room ladder exists and it is good:** *the staff trusts you → just a guy in the building
→ a problem they are managing → the staff has stopped defending you.* In this career I watched it
move **three times in about twelve decisions**, and watching it move was the most engaging thing that
happened to me in three careers. **Which makes career 2 the indictment:** the same ladder, fed six
"cost you" verdicts across forty decisions, moved **once, on the last click.** From inside the game
it is impossible to tell when the sentence will move the phrase and when it will not.

### Live notes, seasons 3–5

- Transferred to **San Diego State**, then **TCU** for a fifth year. **My locker-room standing came
  with me both times** — "the staff has stopped defending you" survived a change of program with no
  reset and no acknowledgement that the new staff had never met me.
- **On the Sideline, On Camera** — a three-option card (rare and welcome). Owned it on the record:
  **"Earned you credit in the locker room,"** and the tracker moved **back up**, "the staff has
  stopped defending you" → "a problem they are managing." **This was the best moment of the session.**
  A thing I broke, that I could see was broken, that I then fixed, and watched get better. It is the
  single clearest proof that the underlying system works.
- **Your Team Now**, again: *"You set the tone on the first series and the rest follow."* I am a
  **kicker**, a first-year transfer, and the header two inches above says the staff has stopped
  defending me. Three separate contradictions in one four-line card.
- **Bed Check** (three options). Told the coach first: *"He respects that you came to him first,"*
  → **"Earned you credit in the locker room"** and **−3 OVR.** Later, **The Portal Rumor**: shut it
  down in writing → **"Earned you credit in the locker room"** and **−3 OVR.** And **Skip the
  Install**: did the podcast → **"Cost you in the locker room"** and **+1 OVR.** Neither of those
  last three carried a "THIS CARD / EVERYTHING ELSE" split.
- **Family Business arrives a second time, verbatim**, two seasons after the same cousin lost half my
  NIL money in "an opportunity". The card does not know it already happened.
- **"You Start Counting"** (the SR "more of these behind you than ahead" beat) fired in season 4 —
  but my redshirt meant I still had a whole season left. The last-year framing arrived a year early.
- **Senior Day** fired at TCU, a school I had joined in August: *"Last time you run out of this
  tunnel."*
- **"The Last One"** — a bespoke **fifth-year-senior** opener. The header class label reads
  **"5TH-YR SR"** and is correct. The game does handle the redshirt path properly.
- One outcome named its own cause well: **"A season on the sideline cost you ground"** (−1), on a
  season where I only played 65 snaps.

### Kicker ending screen (C.3)

**"Undrafted"** in large gold with **"No team called."** beneath it. In careers 1 and 2 the draft

### C. The Kicker — a different career, or a thinner one?

1. **A different career, and — surprisingly — the *best-served* position I played.** The kicker got
   **five** bespoke cards: *Kickoff Duties Too* ("same leg, twice the workload, same paycheck"),
   *The Wind at Home*, *Bowl Game Winner*, *The Fake*, *The Long Attempt*. My offensive-skill career
   got **one** bespoke card in four seasons. My defensive back got three. On top of that the kicker
   has his own stat vocabulary (FG / Points / **Game-Winners**) and his own moments. **Where it
   showed up, it was excellent.** Where it did not: the generic cards are jarring in a way they are
   not for other positions — *"ON THE FIELD: in the rotation"* sat on a kicker's header for five
   seasons; *Rivalry Week* asked a placekicker to play through a barking ankle four times; *Your Team
   Now* had him setting the tone on the first series; *Somebody Rolls Up On You* had a teammate fall
   across his leg; *On the Sideline, On Camera* had him "sit out the next series". **It reads as a
   different career interrupted by a generic one, not as a smaller one.**
2. **Did it ever feel like nothing mattered?** Mostly no — this was the career where I could see the
   most. The redshirt worked and was visible (9 snaps, +5 OVR, a fifth timeline row). The
   locker-room ladder moved four times and I could watch it. **The one place it badly failed: the
   Bowl Game Winner.** I missed a 40-yard kick to win a bowl game — the biggest single moment the
   game has — and the outcome was **THIS CARD −3 / EVERYTHING ELSE +3**, so **the number in the
   header read 60 before the kick and 60 after it.** The most dramatic thing that can happen to a
   kicker moved nothing I could see. (It did survive to the ending screen as a Bowl Win ×2 chip's
   absence and a "Senior Day did not go to script"-style moment, but not at the time.)
3. **Ending screen:** covered above. Loudest element **"Undrafted"**, and it matched.

## B. Career 1, the straight one — is playing well its own reward?

1. **Did it ever feel like it paid off?** Yes, twice, and both were *football* moments, not character
   ones. **Decision 11, Going Home for the Summer:** stayed and trained → *"+1 this card, +8
   everything else — An injury healed and gave the points back,"* and ON THE FIELD moved **"in the
   rotation" → "a week-one starter."** A thing I had chosen four decisions earlier came back, was
   named, and changed my status line. **Decision 28:** playing through Rivalry Week pushed me to
   **"one of the best in the conference."** Those two moments are why I wanted a second career.
   **Working hard on the *character* cards paid nothing, ever.** "Skip the podcast, be in the room" →
   a sentence and no number. "Have someone read it first" on the sportsbook NIL deal → a sentence and
   no number. Not even a "no change" row. The clean path is flat.
2. **Was the selfish option ever obviously better?** Once, hard, and it is the highest-value card on
   this page — **The Watch List.** Career 1, "Never mention it": *"You are the only one in the room
   not talking about it, which somehow becomes its own story."* → **"Cost you in the locker room,"**
   and it dropped me a tier. Career 2, "Put the graphic up": *"You post it in July and then go play
   like a man who belongs on the list."* → **+1 OVR, no cost.** **Humility was punished and
   self-promotion was paid, on the same card.** *(Honest caveat, and I want it on the record: in
   career 3 "Put the graphic up" gave −1 and a locker-room cost, so this is a roll, not a rule. But
   a player who plays those two careers in that order will conclude it is a rule, and so did I, for
   two hours.)* The broader pattern is firmer than the single card: **"Enjoy the trip" cost me
   nothing on all four occasions I took it**, while **"Treat it like a playoff game" cost me in the
   locker room twice.** The shirking option was the only reliably free one in the game.
3. **Did my standing move, and did I notice?** It moved **once in four seasons**, at decision 3, and
   I noticed only because it was the first feedback of the career. After that: three explicit
   verdicts, zero movement, and the ending screen never mentioned it. I noticed nothing — because
   there was nothing to notice.

## THE CLAIMS — confirm / refute

**1. Getting caught costs you something you can see. — PARTIAL.**
When you are caught, the game *says so* at that instant, every time, in gold: *"The bed check catches
an empty room"* → "Cost you in the locker room," −1 OVR. That part is flawless. But "something you
can see" is only true when the hidden meter happens to cross a tier boundary. In career 2 I was
caught **six** times and the visible indicator changed **once**, on the last click of the career.
Across three careers: **13 "Cost you in the locker room" verdicts, 4 visible changes.**

**2. The card's number agrees with its words. — REFUTED.** Four instances, all with the card's *own*
number (no "EVERYTHING ELSE" label to explain them away):
- **The Group Chat** (C2 D22): *"...The coordinator remembers it anyway." / "Cost you in the locker
  room."* → **+1 OVR, now 62.**
- **Skip the Install** (C3): *"Good episode, real exposure. You piece the install together from a
  teammate's notes." / "Cost you in the locker room."* → **+1 OVR, now 71.**
- **Bed Check** (C3): *"He respects that you came to him first..." / "Earned you credit in the locker
  room."* → **−3 OVR, now 64.**
- **The Portal Rumor** (C3): *"You spend the week proving you are not going anywhere." / "Earned you
  credit in the locker room."* → **−3 OVR, now 70.**
Plus a fifth of a different kind, in **all three careers**: **Fall Camp Battle** offers exactly one
button, labelled **"Win the job"**, and then resolves *"You press, and pressing looks exactly like
panicking on film," −1 OVR.* **The only thing I could click asserted an outcome the card then denied.**
And a sixth: **Going Home for the Summer** (C3) — *"You come back lighter than you left. Some of that
is rest and some of it is nobody watching"* → **"Earned you credit in the locker room."**

Two of these are defensible (rating and standing are different axes), but from the seat they read as
the number contradicting the sentence, and nothing on the card explains that they are different axes.

**3. You always know where you are. — PARTIAL.** Season, phase and class are always on screen and
always right; the timeline is genuinely good; no button ever looked clickable when it wasn't. Two
real defects:
- **Year five is labelled three ways** across two screens: header "5TH-YR SR", ending table "Y5",
  ending MOMENTS "SR" — producing two consecutive "SR" seasons in the same list.
- **Snap counts disagree** between the header and the timeline for the current season (header
  "344 snaps" vs Year 3 row "369 snaps"). The timeline's only disclaimer is "OVR BEFORE THE
  OFFSEASON", which covers the OVR column and nothing else.
- Not a defect but a real gap: **there is no injury or health state anywhere in the UI.** I played
  eight decisions in a walking boot across three careers with nothing on screen saying so.

**4. Each ending matches its own career. — CONFIRMED,** for each career judged on its own.
Career 1 (clean, 84 OVR, one of the best in the conference): Round 4 — right, though nothing in four
seasons taught me what OVR buys at the draft, so the number landed as an unauditable verdict.
Career 2 (72 OVR, two ankle injuries): Round 7 — right.
Career 3 (kicker, 67 OVR): Undrafted — right and honest.
**The caveat is what the endings do *not* contain:** none of the three mentions the locker room in
any form, including career 2, whose final on-screen state was *"a problem they are managing."*

**5. The program's opinion of you is something you track. — REFUTED as stated, and this is the
finding I would act on first.** The phrase exists, the ladder is excellent writing —
*the staff trusts you → just a guy in the building → a problem they are managing → the staff has
stopped defending you* — and when it moves it is the best thing in the game. **But:** it moved once
in 40 decisions in career 2, and four times in ~35 in career 3, with no way from inside to tell
which. It is **not on the ending screen at all**. It is **not highlighted when it changes** — no
arrow, no flash, no colour, nothing; I only ever caught it by having memorised the old words. And
**"Cost you in the locker room" is used as a generic penalty string on cards that have nothing to do
with teammates** — my cousin losing my NIL money "cost me in the locker room". Did I care? **I tried
to for two careers and stopped.** In career 3 I cared again, for exactly one card, because I got to
watch it go back up.

**6. Anything hard to read, cut off, overflowing, or colliding. — NONE FOUND.** Nothing overflowed,
collided or truncated at 1456×824 in ~120 decisions. Two *layout* notes, not defects: the
**"ACROSS N SEASONS"** summary is pinned to the bottom of the right column, ~450px below the last
timeline row in an otherwise empty column, so it reads as detached from the thing it summarises; and
on cards that resolve to nothing, **"No change"** is rendered in the gold accent, making a null
result the loudest element on the card.

## WHAT TO REPORT

- **Where I got bored / lost.** Career 1, **decision ~28** — Rivalry Week's third verbatim
  appearance. From there I stopped reading card bodies and read only option labels. By career 3 I was
  clicking repeat cards by button position without reading either. **Verbatim repeats I counted:**
  Rivalry Week ×4 in one career (and it opened with "Your ankle has been barking since Tuesday" every
  single time), Bowl Season ×4, Fall Camp Battle ×3, The Group Chat ×2, Redshirt? ×3, Family Business
  ×2, Bed Check ×2, Transfer Portal every season.
- **Arbitrary choices.** The kicker's **Signing Day** — Toledo / UC Davis / James Madison with no
  hint on any of them. The other careers had "— the two-deep is thin" on at least one school; here
  there was nothing to choose on. Also **Family Business** and **The Watch List**, whose options
  point at no axis the game has told me it tracks.
- **Invisible that should be visible:** injury/health state; the locker-room meter's actual position
  between tiers; what OVR is worth at the draft.
- **Prominent that did not deserve it:** "No change" in the accent colour; the four-times-a-career
  "Carried the team through bowl season" moment, which is most of career 1's moments list.
- **What made me want to play again:** the per-season delta column (56 → 72 → 80 → 83), MOMENTS, the
  position-specific cards, and — once — watching the locker-room line climb back after I fixed it.
- **What made me want to stop:** being told six times that something cost me, and never being shown
  the bill.

> **The single thing most in the way of a fourth career:** *the game tells me in words that it is
> counting my character and then never shows me the ledger — not while I play, and not on the
> ending screen.*

### The curve, for the record

> **yes → no, but I did it because you asked → no.**

Which is the fifth previous playtester's curve with one extra career on it, and I got there for the
same stated reason. **But I want to put a correction next to it, because I do not think the sentence
"I stopped believing the game is counting what I do" is true any more.** The game *is* counting: the

made, and the delta column tracked me season by season. **Every one of those is the game counting,
and I could see every one of them.** What is broken is narrower and more fixable: **the one track the
game talks about most — the locker room — is the one it shows me least, and it is completely absent
from the screen that is supposed to sum the career up.**
