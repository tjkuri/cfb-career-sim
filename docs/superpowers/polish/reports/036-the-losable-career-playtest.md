## A. Could you see it going wrong — while it was still going wrong?

### The in-the-moment notes from career 2

I played Ratliff as a lazy, self-serving player: every low-effort option, every duck, every me-first
answer. These lines were written as I went, not afterwards.

- **Decision 1 — Signing Day.** Three schools, no descriptors on any of them. No record, no level, no
  depth-chart note. I picked the one whose name sounded smallest. Nothing to choose on.
- **Decision 2 — The Position Switch.** Refused the move. *"Cost you in the locker room."* and the
  header line changed from **"the staff trusts you"** to **"just a guy in the building."** First visible
  ding, and it came fast.
- **Decision 4 — Bubble Screen Diet.** Took the lazy option ("Take what's given"). Got *"the staff
  notices the professionalism"* and **"Earned you credit in the locker room"** — the label went back
  **up** to "the staff trusts you." The lazy play was praised. If I were trying to play badly, the game
  just told me I was doing fine.
- **Decision 8 — end of season 1.** "Enjoy the trip" at the bowl printed **nothing at all** — no
  number, no locker-room line, no consequence. Season-end **61**, which is exactly what my honest
  kicker had after his freshman year. Nothing on screen says this career is different.
- **Decision 10 — Redshirt?** The instruction said duck the field, so I did. **+5 OVR**, on the card
  that offers it, before the season. The biggest single gain in either career, for choosing to sit out.
- **Decision 12.** Snaps read **7**. The redshirt is real and visible in the header. Good.
- **Decision 13 — The Watch List.** "Put the graphic up." **−1 OVR** plus a locker-room cost. First time
  a number moved against me, 13 decisions in.
- **Decision 16 — end of season 2.** **67**, and the rail says *"+6, up 6 on the season before."* My
  honest kicker was 69 at this point. Two points apart.
- **Decision 18 — Fall Camp Battle.** One button. Free **+2**. And I was promoted to **"a week-one
  starter"** after a 23-snap season. Nothing I did was weighed.
- **Decision 20 — Your Team Now.** **IN THE LOCKER ROOM now reads "a problem they are managing."**
  That is the first line anywhere on this screen that sounds like a career in trouble. OVR is 69 and
  still climbing. *This is the moment I first knew.*
- **Decision 21 — The NIL Deal Nobody Read.** Took the sportsbook money. *"Cost you in the locker
  room."* and **"+2 OVR"** on the same card. The big number went up.
- **Decision 26 — end of season 3.** **72**, *"+5, up 5 on the season before."* 823 scrimmage yards, 8
  TD, 7-5. By the numbers this is a good junior year.
- **Decision 30 — Skip the Install.** Locker room drops again, to **"the staff has stopped defending
  you."** Fourth rung down, and the language is genuinely alarming. Meanwhile OVR ticked up to **76**,
  my highest of the career.
- **Decision 32 — The Ticket Hookup.** Sold my comps; *"Compliance flags the resale listing with your
  name still in the metadata."* Result: *"Cost you in the locker room."* **+1 OVR**, and **ON THE FIELD
  promoted to "one of the best in the conference."** I got caught breaking NCAA rules and both big
  status readouts improved.
- **Decision 34 — end of season 4.** **77**, *"+5"* for the fourth straight year. Team went 3-9; my
  line did not care.
- **Decision 36 — Going Home for the Summer.** Skipped summer training. *"You come back lighter than
  you left. Some of that is rest and some of it is nobody watching."* → **"Earned you credit in the
  locker room."**
- **Decision ~40 — The Exit Interview.** *"The staff sits you down… and tells you exactly where you
  stand. It is not where you thought."* This is the only card in five seasons that says out loud that
  the career is in trouble — and it arrives in the postseason of the **final** season, with nothing
  left to act on.
- **Final ledger.** 81 season-end / 83 final. **61 → 81, +20 across 5 seasons.** Every single year in
  the rail carries a green "+N". There is no bad year in the ladder.

### The five questions, answered plainly

**1. Did you know your career was going badly before the ending screen told you?**

**Partly — and on the thing that actually matters, no.** I knew my *standing in the building* was bad.
I did not know the *career* was going badly, because it wasn't: the ending confirmed it. I went into
the ending screen expecting to be drafted, and I was.

**2. At roughly which decision number did you first know?**

**Decision 20**, when the locker-room line hit "a problem they are managing." (The first ding was
decision 2, but at that point it reads as one bad day, not a trajectory.)

**3. What told you? Name the exact element.**

One element and one only: the **IN THE LOCKER ROOM** line in the header. It walked down a four-rung
ladder over the career, verbatim:

> "the staff trusts you" → "just a guy in the building" → "a problem they are managing" → "the staff has stopped defending you"

That is the entire warning system. It is not a vibe — it is specific, well-written, and it works. But
it is one short line, set in the smallest, lowest-contrast type in the header block, sitting directly
beneath a 60px OVR number that was going **up** the whole time. Everything else on the screen — the
OVR, the yardage, the TDs, the record, the season ladder with its green "+5"s, the "ACROSS 5 SEASONS
61 → 81 +20" summary — told me the career was going well.

**4. Did you ever see a number go down? Did words explain it at the moment?**

Yes, and yes, every time — the moment-of-change reporting is good.

- Career 2: **−1 OVR** three times (The Watch List; Fall Camp Battle in seasons 4 and 5), each with a
  sentence at the moment: *"You post it in July and spend the whole fall being measured against it"*,
  *"You press, and pressing looks exactly like panicking on film."*
- Career 1: **−5 OVR** twice from injuries, each with *"Overall down 5, now 56."* **and** the excellent
  line *"The OVR comes back when the injury heals."* That is the clearest mechanical explanation in the
  game. It also pays off: the refund arrived later with *"An injury healed and gave the points back."*
- Career 1: **−1 OVR** twice on the Onside Kick card.

The failure is not per-card; it is cumulative. **No season-end number ever went down, in either
career.** The rail printed a green "+N" for all nine seasons I played, including five deliberately
sabotaged ones.

**5. When you reached the ending: were you surprised?**

**Yes — surprised upward, which is the wrong direction.** I expected to be drafted, somewhere mid-to-late,
and Round 4 Pick 30 is roughly that. What I did not expect was the **HONORS: All-American** panel — a
boxed, gold, left-ruled block, the single most prominent element on the page under the round and pick.
Plus All-Conference and a Bowl Win.

**Does that result match what the game had been telling me along the way?** Almost exactly, and that is
the problem. The game had been telling me, in every number it printed, that this was a good career: +6,
+5, +5, +4, 959 scrimmage yards, 11 TDs, "one of the best in the conference." The one dissenting voice
was the locker-room line, and the ending overruled it. What I had been reading as reassuring — the OVR
climb and the all-green ladder — turned out to be *correct*. The ending was **better than the one bad
signal**, not worse than the good ones.

**6. Was there ever a point where you thought "I should change how I'm playing"?**

Twice, both triggered by the same element: **decision 20** ("a problem they are managing") and
**decision 30** ("the staff has stopped defending you"). Both times the prompt was the locker-room
line and nothing else. Following the brief I kept going, but I note honestly that I *wanted* to change
at both points — which is a genuine win for that one line, and the strongest thing in this report's
favour.

## B. The Kicker — a different career, or a thinner one?

**Different in a real but narrow band; generic everywhere else.**

**1. Where the kicker showed up, and where it did not.**

It showed up in two places, and both are good:

- **The stat line.** "5 FG · 23 Points", later "20 FG · 87 Points · 1 Game-Winner". It is a kicker's
  ledger, not a repainted one, and "Game-Winner" appearing right after the card that caused it is the
  best small moment in the build.
- **Four cards.** *Kickoff Duties Too* ("Same leg, twice the workload, same paycheck"), *Bowl Game
  Winner*, *Onside Kick* (twice). These are written for a kicker and nobody else.

Where it did not, and where it actively broke:

- **"Next Man Up"** — *"The guy in front of you goes down in warmups… Take every snap and do not come
  off."* I had just gone into a boot on the previous card. A kicker does not take "every snap."
- **"Somebody Rolls Up On You"** — *"Get up and finish the drive."* A kicker is not in the pile.
- **The entire offseason.** Redshirt? / Transfer Portal / Fall Camp Battle / New Workout Regimen /
  Family Business / The Weight Room Bet — every one of them position-neutral, all four offseasons.
- **Transfer Portal option text:** *"Transfer to Georgia Southern (bigger role)"*. A kicker has one
  role. There is no bigger one.

**2. Did it ever feel like nothing you did mattered?**

Mostly no — OVR moved when I expected and sat still when I expected. Three exceptions:

- **The same choice gave opposite results.** "Bowl Season → Treat it like a playoff game" paid **+2 OVR**
  twice, then in season 3 paid *"Cost you in the locker room"* with the same words on the option.
- **"Cutting the Line → Wait your turn like everyone else"** → *"Nobody notices, which is fine."*
  Literally nothing happened. A choice with no consequence in either direction.
- **"Fall Camp Battle"** has one button and hands out **+2** for free.

**3. Did it feel easier than it should?**

**Yes, and the ending is the proof.** A kicker went **Round 3, Pick 8, #72 overall.** Kickers do not go
in the third round. The route there was frictionless: take the extra-work option on every card, collect
+1/+2 each time, 61 → 86, no season ever going backwards. The moment it registered was the end of
season 4 — "+9 on the season before" on a 4-8 team, arriving with no resistance at all.

**4. Was the ending in line with how you thought the career had gone?**

Yes, directionally — but it under-celebrated relative to the career that deserved it less. See claim 3.

**One-line verdict on the Kicker: it is a real kicker in its stat line and about four cards, and a
generic skill player everywhere else — especially the entire offseason — and it is too easy: a Round 3
kicker is not a thing.**

## C. The offseason — a beat, or a loading screen?

**1. What happens between seasons, as a player?**

Nothing marks it. You click **CONTINUE** on the last card of a season and land on a card in the next
season. There is no screen between seasons. The only things that tell you a year passed are the word
**OFFSEASON** in the sub-header, the class year ticking FR → SO, and a new row appearing in the right
rail — a rail I had stopped reading by season 3.

The offseason *is* two to five ordinary decision cards, drawn from a fixed set: **Redshirt?** (years 2
and 3 only), **Transfer Portal**, **Fall Camp Battle**, **New Workout Regimen**, plus one life card
(Family Business / The Weight Room Bet / The Portal Rumor / Going Home for the Summer). Same cards,
same order, both careers.

**2. A moment in the career, or clicking past a screen?**

**Neither — it is less than a loading screen,** because a loading screen at least announces itself.
The transition from career 1 season 1 to season 2 was one click from the "Bowl Game Winner" result
straight into "Redshirt?". My best season-1 moment and the start of season 2 were one button apart.

**3. Did the later offseasons feel different from the early ones?**

**No. Identical.** Career 1's season 3 → 4 offseason ran Transfer Portal then Fall Camp Battle — the
exact pair from season 1 → 2. Career 2's fifth-year offseason ran Transfer Portal then Fall Camp Battle
again. The stakes never rose. The only escalation I felt anywhere was **in-season**: the season-opener
ladder (The First One → No Longer New → Your Team Now → You Start Counting → The Last One) and the
excellent **"5TH-YR SR"** label. The offseason contributed nothing to either.

**4. If you could add exactly one thing to the gap between two seasons:**

**A season recap beat — one screen, unskippable-by-accident, between the last card of a season and the
first card of the next.** Record, my stat line, where I stand with the staff in a sentence, and what is
at stake next year. The data already exists; it currently appears in the right rail *behind* a card I
am busy reading, which is why I click past it. Put it in the middle of the screen once a year and the
offseason becomes a beat.

## Claims to confirm or refute

### 1. A bad career now visibly fails — **REFUTED**

By its third season career 2 read: **72 OVR**, *"+5, up 5 on the season before"*, 823 scrimmage yards,
8 TD, 7-5. Career 1 at the same point read 77. **Five points apart**, after three seasons in which I
took the worst available option on every single card. It ended **drafted in Round 4** with an
**All-American** honour. The only thing I can point at that distinguishes the two careers is one phrase
in the header: "the staff has stopped defending you."

The mechanism that should carry this — the locker-room ladder — is real and well written, but it is
sealed off from everything else on screen. It never touches the OVR, never touches the season ladder,
never touches the draft slot, and never touches the honours.

### 2. Sitting out a season is a real decision — **REFUTED as stated**

- **Offered in Year 2 (SO)** in both careers, and **again in Year 3 (JR)** in career 1 — identical text
  both times.
- It read as an **obvious free yes**. It paid **+5 OVR immediately**, resolved on the card that offers
  it, *before* the season is played, with prose that has already fast-forwarded a year: *"You come back
  bigger, and the coaches notice in the first padded practice."*
- **Nothing told me what I was giving up** in any number. The card says *"a gamble on coming back
  stronger — it might backfire."* It did not backfire. The cost only surfaced later and quietly, as
  **7 snaps** and then **23 snaps** in the header.
- The one genuine upside is handled well: it bought a fifth year, correctly and clearly labelled
  **"5TH-YR SR"**.

### 3. The ending screen ranks honestly — **REFUTED. Sharpest finding in the report.**

**The badly-played career's ending is the more congratulatory of the two.**

- Career 2 (five seasons of ducking, cheating and quitting) gets a **HONORS** section containing
  **All-American** — gold text, boxed, left-ruled, the most prominent element on the page below the
  round and pick — plus "ALSO EARNED: All-Conference · Bowl Win".
- Career 1 (played straight, drafted a full round higher) gets **no HONORS block at all** — only the
  smaller grey "ALSO EARNED" chips.

So the exact test in the brief — *could you rank your endings at a glance, without reading the round
and pick numbers?* — **fails, and inverts**. At a glance, career 2 is clearly the better career. It is
a round worse. The offending element is the **HONORS panel**: it is the loudest thing on the screen and
it is keyed to something (an All-American season) that my worst-behaved career earned and my best-behaved
one did not.

### 4. You always know where you are — **PARTIAL**

Good: `SEASON 4 · SR` top-right, `OFFSEASON / NON-CONFERENCE / CONFERENCE PLAY / POSTSEASON` in the
sub-header, last season's record beside it, and **"5TH-YR SR"** — which is exactly right and I never
had to think about it.

Two failures:

- **The vanishing Year 5.** The right rail shows five year slots for the whole career. Career 1 ended
  after **four** seasons and went straight to the ending screen. Nothing ever told me why year 5 existed
  in the rail, or why I didn't get one. I only understood after career 2, where a redshirt unlocked it.
- **The rail runs ahead of the header.** On the last card of every season, the rail highlights
  **YEAR N+1** while the header still reads `SEASON N · POSTSEASON`. Two different answers to "where am
  I" on one screen.

### 5. The card's number agrees with its words — **REFUTED. Four instances, all the same direction.**

Every one of these is a card whose prose says I did something bad and whose number rewards me:

| Card | The sentence | The number |
|---|---|---|
| The Group Chat (c1) | *"The coordinator does not think it is funny."* / **"Cost you in the locker room."** | **+1 OVR** — "Overall up 1, now 71." |
| The NIL Deal Nobody Read (c2) | *"Compliance never calls."* (a sportsbook deal, as an amateur) / **"Cost you in the locker room."** | **+2 OVR** — "now 71." |
| The Ticket Hookup (c2) | *"Compliance flags the resale listing with your name still in the metadata."* / **"Cost you in the locker room."** | **+1 OVR**, *and* ON THE FIELD promoted to **"one of the best in the conference."** |
| Going Home for the Summer (c2) | *"You come back lighter than you left. Some of that is rest and some of it is nobody watching."* | **"Earned you credit in the locker room."** |

Two more of a different kind:

- **The header and the rail disagree about OVR on the same screen, at every season boundary.** Career 1,
  end of season 1: the card says *"Everything else: overall up 2, **now 63**"*, the header reads **63**,
  and eight inches to the right, under the heading **OVR AT SEASON'S END**, the Year 1 row reads **61**.
  Same at every boundary: 69 vs 71, 77 vs 78, 86 vs 89. A player reading the rail is reading a number
  two-to-three points stale under a label that claims to be current.
- **Arithmetic, still present:** "Bowl Game Winner" says *"your team down two"* and offers
  **"Take the tie and send it to overtime."** Down two, a field goal **wins**. There is no tie available.

### 6. Anything hard to read, cut off, overflowing or colliding — **one real defect**

Nothing is broken, overflowing or colliding at 1456px. Type and contrast are fine.

The one actual defect is on the **create screen**: **START THE RECRUITING CYCLE** renders in full solid
gold at full opacity — visually identical to its enabled state — while it is `disabled` because
**LAST NAME** is empty. Nothing marks LAST NAME as required. I clicked the button twice and nothing
happened, with no feedback of any kind.

Layout note, not a defect: at 1456px the app occupies a ~1020px band, the decision card is ~480px wide,
and the bottom ~40% of the page is empty black.

## What to report

**Where I got bored, confused or lost.** Bored at **career 1 decision ~31** — Rivalry Week, its third
verbatim appearance inside one career. From there I stopped reading it and just clicked "Sit and get
right," because I had learned "Play through it" costs −5 and the locker-room penalty for sitting had
no visible effect. The repeated card taught me its own dominant answer.

Repeat counts **within single careers**: Rivalry Week **×4** (c1) and **×5** (c2). Bowl Season ×4 and
×5. Fall Camp Battle ×3 and ×3. The Group Chat ×2. You Got Catfished ×2 — being catfished by the same
bit twice is funny once and then it is a bug.

**Did a choice feel arbitrary?** Career 2's **Signing Day**: three schools, no record, no level, no
depth-chart note, nothing. Career 1's at least said *"the two-deep is thin."* Also **"Saturday Night,
Everybody Watching"** — "Play like it is a Tuesday practice" is simultaneously the lazy answer and the
disciplined-veteran answer, and I could not tell which one the game thought it was.

**Invisible that should be visible:** the locker-room meter. *"Cost you in the locker room"* printed at
least a dozen times across the two careers while the label did not move. Career 1 sat on "just a guy in
the building" for roughly 25 straight decisions while being told four separate times that something had
cost it. There is plainly a number under that phrase and I can never see it — which is exactly the
number that was supposed to tell me the career was going wrong.

**Prominent that did not deserve it:** **Fall Camp Battle**. One button, labelled "Win the job." It ran
six times across two careers, dispenses +2 (or −1) with no decision taken, and **re-labels your ON THE
FIELD status regardless of the season before** — it promoted my 23-snap sabotage career to "a week-one
starter."

**What I was skimming (career 2, since career 3 was not played).** By season 3 I was reading exactly
two things per card: the header's two status lines, and the bold effect line (**+2 OVR** / **Cost you
in the locker room**). I had stopped reading: the card's scene-setting paragraph, the outcome prose,
the entire right-hand rail, and the *"A season of development came in."* line.

**What made me want to play again:** after career 1, wanting to see whether a non-kicker got different
cards.

**What made me want to stop:** the All-American.

**One line — the single thing most in the way of a fourth career:**

> I played five seasons as a cheat, a quitter and a guy compliance caught, and the game handed me
> All-American in gold and a fourth-round pick — so my choices are not being counted, and a fourth
> career would just be a fourth slideshow.

## Best moment in the build

The locker-room ladder, decisions 20 and 30 of career 2: **"a problem they are managing"** followed by
**"the staff has stopped defending you."** Those two phrases are the only time in nine seasons that the
game made me want to change how I was playing. They are doing all of the work this wave was aiming at —
and they are doing it alone, in the smallest type on the screen, with every other readout arguing the
opposite.
