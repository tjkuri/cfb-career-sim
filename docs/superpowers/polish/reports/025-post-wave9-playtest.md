## 2. The skimming question — onset or dose

**Verdict: ONSET, decisively — with a per-career reset that does not survive the first card.**

Within career 1, exactly **one** repeat flipped me, and the eleven repeats that followed did not
degrade me further; they simply held me there. Across careers there is no dose curve at all, because
the very first card of every new career is a repeat, so careers 2, 3 and 4 all began at full skim on
**decision 1**.

Dose shows up in exactly one place: it governs **how long a reversal lasts**. A new, well-written,
position-specific card pulls me back into full reading for **one card**, and then I am gone again.

### Career 1 — Quarterback, 41 decisions

1. **Decision 15.**
2. **Rivalry Week** — a *verbatim* repeat of decision 5 (same body, same two options).
3. **A specific moment.** I read the title and the first six words ("Nobody on campus has talked
   about anything else…"), recognised the barking ankle, and my eyes jumped straight to the buttons.
   It was not gradual; it was a snap.
4. **It reversed twice, briefly.** Decision 16 (*The Ticket Hookup*, brand-new, genuinely good) I read
   in full. Decision 27 (*Somebody Rolls Up On You* — "A teammate just falls the wrong way across the
   back of your leg, and the whole sideline hears it") pulled me all the way back in, arriving right
   after a −5. Neither held. **By decision 23 (*New Playbook*, a card I had never seen) I was reading
   the title and the two options only.** Eight decisions from first repeat-skim to skimming
   brand-new cards.

### Career 2 — Kicker, 34 decisions

1. **Decision 1.**
2. **Signing Day** — a repeat from career 1. I read zero words of it.
3. Specific: seeing "Signing Day / Where do you commit?" a second time.
4. **Reversed constantly and substantially — kicker cards were the single strongest reading-restorer
   in the whole session.** *New Holder* (D2), *Onside Kick*, *The Chip Shot*, *The Wind at Home*,
   *The Yips*, *The Long Attempt* — I read every one of those in full and enjoyed them. Everything
   generic (*The Watch List* ×3, *Rivalry Week* ×3, *Bowl Season* ×3, *Fall Camp Battle* ×2) I
   clicked without reading a word.

### Career 3 — Defensive line, 22 decisions

1. **Decision 1**, and it never really lifted.
2. A repeat (*Signing Day* again).
3. Specific, but by career 3 the "moment" is just starting the game.
4. **Three reversals**: *Weight Class* and *Gap Discipline* (both DL-specific), and — the strongest
   reversal of the whole session — the **Declare for the Draft?** screen, which I read twice and then
   screenshotted. **Fear restored my reading, not the writing.** I was about to take a gamble and I
   was hunting the card for information about the odds. It had none, which is finding #2 below.

### Career 4 — Offensive skill, 38 decisions

1. **Decision 1.**
2. A repeat.
3. Specific.
4. Reversed for *Return Duty*, *The Toe-Tap*, *Bubble Screen Diet*, *The Five-Star Behind You*,
   *The Position Switch* — all position cards, all one card deep. **Around decision 30 I stopped
   reading the outcome prose as well**, which I had still been reading up to that point. By season 4
   I was reading three things and nothing else: the two option buttons, the OVR number, and the delta.

### The mechanism, since you asked for feel not code

Two things trained me, and they compound:

- **The same card, same choice, different outcome.** *Rivalry Week / Play through it* gave +2, +2,
  then −5. Once I learned the text does not predict the result, reading it stopped paying.
- **The prose and the number routinely disagree** (six exact instances in §8 below). Once the words
  are decorative and the number is the game, reading the words is a tax.

Repetition is what starts it. **The text/number contradictions are what make it rational to stay
that way,** and they apply to new cards as much as repeated ones — which is exactly how it
generalises to cards I have never seen.

## 3. Claims to confirm or refute

### 1. Could you lose? — **CONFIRMED, strongly**

Career 3 ended: **"Undrafted"** / *"No team called."* That is a real ending and the line is excellent.

But answer the second half honestly: **it never felt like failure was on the table while I was
playing.** I picked badly on purpose for four straight decisions in season 1–2 and my OVR went
**56 → 65**. The floor I actually touched was 54. I did not work out that failure was possible — I
found out *after it happened*, on the results screen. There is a losing **outcome** now; there is
still no losing **pressure**. Nothing during play told me I was near a line.

What it did to the ending: it was by far the most memorable of my four, and it is the reason I
started career 4 at all.

### 2. Declaring early — **TRAP, not a gamble. REFUTED as acceptable.**

The card, in full: *"You're draft-eligible. Cash in now, or return for another season?"* plus one
line of small grey type: *"Two seasons of eligibility remain."* Then two buttons.

No projected round. No stock indicator. No warning language. No "you are currently projected as X."
**"Cash in now" is upside framing** — it is the phrase you use for collecting winnings.

I knew it was risky only from real-world football knowledge, not from anything the game told me, and
I only learned the cost on the results screen with no reference point to compare it to. **It reads as
a trap I was walked into.** The one change that would flip it to a gamble: put a projection on the
card, and let the player watch it move.

### 3. The transfer — **PARTIAL. "Menu" is still the right word.**

It now produces a card, which is more than before. But the card is **quieter than an ordinary
decision resolution**: a small "Boise State → Fresno State" arrow row, the flat sentence "Moved from
Boise State to Fresno State.", and on career 4's first transfer **no OVR delta at all**. The header
behind the card still said "Boise State". *Bowl Trip Curfew* — a throwaway card about sneaking out of
a hotel — has more visual weight than changing schools.

**When did I find out what the transfer did to me?** Career 1: at the *year-end ledger row*, roughly
eight decisions later, when 5-7 became 9-3. Never from the transfer itself.

**The belief I formed, and what gave it to me:**

> "Bigger role" = +5 OVR, reliably. "Transfer up (tougher competition)" = nothing at all. "Stay" =
> a random unnarrated jackpot — I saw +3, +5 and +10.

Every word of that came from **watching the gold OVR number tick**, not from a single word the game
wrote. And note the worst of it: **"Stay at Ole Miss" paid +10 OVR — the largest single swing in my
entire session — with zero text. No sentence at all.** The most powerful lever in the game is the one
with no narration on it.

### 4. The draft ending — **PARTIAL, and yes, the bad career reads as congratulatory**

**Could I rank two endings at a glance without reading the numbers?** No. Round 5 Pick 14 vs Round 5
Pick 2 required reading both pick numbers *and* knowing that a lower pick is better — and to a
non-expert "Pick 14" intuitively reads as more than "Pick 2". Round 4 vs Round 5 needs the same
inverted knowledge. Only "Undrafted" was instantly rankable, and it is rankable in the wrong
direction:

**The exact element: the word "Undrafted" is set in the identical large gold display type, at the
identical size, in the identical position as "Round 5, Pick 14" and "Round 4, Pick 12".** Gold is the
game's celebration colour — it is the primary button, it is the accent bar on every option. The
failure headline is styled as a trophy.

Reinforced by two more things on the same screen:
- **"ACROSS 2 SEASONS · 55 → 59 · +4"** — a positive growth number, presented as the career summary,
  on a career that failed.
- The **MOMENTS** reel, which for my undrafted player printed 📺 "Made the primetime play they
  replayed all year" and ☀️ "Enjoyed the bowl trip more than the bowl game" ×2. A highlight reel for
  a guy nobody called.

**And the repetition has now reached the ending screen.** Career 4's Moments reel printed
🏆 **"Carried the team through bowl season" four times** — once per season, identically — on a player
who went 6-6, 5-7, 5-7, 4-8 and has no Bowl Win in ALSO EARNED. The Moments reel is the best feature
in the game and it is being spent on the same line four times in a row.

### 5. Position — **costume in the offseason, character in-season. PARTIAL.**

**Where the difference showed up:** in-season cards, and they were the best writing in the build.
*New Holder*, *Onside Kick*, *The Chip Shot*, *The Wind at Home*, *The Yips*, *The Long Attempt* (K);
*Weight Class*, *Gap Discipline* (DL); *The Two-Minute Drill* (QB); *Return Duty*, *The Toe-Tap*,
*Bubble Screen Diet*, *The Position Switch* (OSKILL). The stat lines also differ correctly —
Pass Yds/TD/INT · FG/Points/Game-Winners · Sacks/TFL/Tackles · Scrimmage Yds/TD/Rec. Kicker in
particular felt like a genuinely different person to play.

**Where it did not, plainly: the offseason was identical at all four positions.** Every career, every
year: Redshirt? → Declare for the Draft? → Transfer Portal → Fall Camp Battle → New Workout Regimen.
Same words, same order, same options, regardless of position.

Two generic cards also mis-fit audibly:
- *Your Team Now*, dealt to a **kicker**: "You set the tone on the **first series** and the rest follow."
- *The First One* — "Your **hands** are cold and it is seventy degrees" — dealt to a defensive lineman
  and to an offensive lineman-equivalent alike.
- *Signing Day* offered a **kicker** "Commit to Sacramento State — **the two-deep is thin**". A team
  has one kicker.
- *The Position Switch* literally writes around not knowing what I am: "running back to slot, or slot
  to running back, **whichever one you aren't already**." That is the abstraction admitting itself
  mid-sentence.

### 6. Did you know where you were? — **CONFIRMED**

The header (school / SEASON n / class / phase) and the right-hand year rail were always legible and I
**never lost track of the year or the season.** This is solid.

I did lose track of two other things:
- **Remaining eligibility** — stated only inside the Declare card, nowhere in the HUD. I took a
  redshirt in career 2 and had no way to know it had banked me a year until six decisions later, when
  a *different* card's subordinate clause read "Three seasons of eligibility remain."
- **Whether I was a captain.** *The Captain Vote* told me "the room votes for you anyway" and then
  nothing in the HUD ever mentioned it again until the ending screen.

**And the redshirt does not do what its card says.** "Sit this season" cost me −3 OVR and then I
played the season anyway — same season number, same class, with 5 snaps and 1 FG logged to it.

### 7. The season ledger — **used twice, wallpaper otherwise**

I used it to decide something exactly twice in 135 decisions: comparing 5-7 / 5-7 / **9-3** after the
Ole Miss transfer (which changed how I played the rest of career 1), and glancing at the year rail
before career 2's declare.

**The one time it came alive:** "**1 Game-Winner**" appeared in the stat line immediately after I
recovered an onside kick and tied the game. A stat I could see was *caused by the card I had just
played*. That is the ledger doing its job, and it happened once. Everything else in it — snaps, yards
— moves on its own between cards with no perceivable relationship to my choices, which is why I
stopped watching it.

### 8. Anything hard to read, cut off, overflowing, colliding — **nothing visual; plenty semantic**

At 1456px **nothing clipped, overflowed, or collided, and contrast was fine.** No layout defects to
report.

But the text contradicts the number constantly, and this is the mechanism that made me stop reading.
Exact text, exact delta:

| Card | Outcome text | Delta |
|---|---|---|
| The Position Switch | "The fit never quite comes together, and it shows on the depth chart." | **+5 OVR** |
| The Ticket Hookup | "Compliance flags the resale listing with your name still in the metadata." | **+1 OVR** |
| The Long Attempt | "It's got the leg. It does not have the accuracy — off the upright and away in the wrong direction." | **+1 OVR** |
| Bowl Opt-Out | "You watch from the sideline in a polo. Zero new risk, zero new tape." | **+2 OVR** |
| Cutting the Line | "You eat ten minutes later than you wanted to. Nobody notices, which is fine." | **−3 OVR** |
| Somebody Rolls Up On You | "Sprain, not a tear. They shut you down early and you keep the rest of the season." | **−5 OVR** |

Getting caught by compliance made me better. Missing a 57-yarder made me better. A committed position
switch that "never quite comes together" was the single best offseason result I had all career.

**Cards that assert things the HUD directly contradicts:**
- *Bowl Opt-Out*: "your name's already climbing draft boards" — said to me at **60 OVR, on the scout
  team, as a sophomore.**
- *Playoff or Bust*: "Ten wins is a disappointing season here" — while the ledger beside it read **5-7**.

**Arithmetic and state defects:**
- Ledger read **"8 snaps · 3 FG · 6 Points"**. Three field goals cannot be six points.
- Status read **"ON THE FIELD: on the scout team"** beside **339 snaps and 31 tackles** in the same header.
- The big FINAL OVERALL number never matches the season table's last row (**74** vs 72; **81** vs 79).
  The "after the off-season" caption explains it in ~10px grey and it still reads as a bug every time.
- **Silent resolutions:** "Play this season", "Return to school" and "Stay at [school]" produce **no
  outcome screen at all** — the next card simply appears. The +10 for staying was delivered as a bare
  number on a card with no sentence on it.

### 9. The empty room — **CONFIRMED, badly, at 1456 × 824**

The whole app is a single **~755px band centred in a 1456px window**: roughly **350px of dead black
on the left and 350px on the right**. Vertically it is worse — on a typical decision screen the card
bottom lands around y=560 and the remaining **~260px is empty black**.

**The decision screen uses about a third of the area it has.** It does not read as deliberate
whitespace — the card, the stat header and the year rail all look like a phone layout that was never
given a desktop breakpoint. The year rail in particular sits marooned in the top-right with a huge
void beneath it that would comfortably hold the season stat detail the ending screen already knows
how to lay out.

## 4. What to report

**Where I got bored, confused or lost.** Bored at **career 1 decision 15** (the first Rivalry Week
repeat) and never fully un-bored. The worst single stretch was **career 4, season 2 offseason
(decisions ~14–18)**: five cards in a row I had seen before, in the same order. Lost: only around
eligibility and the redshirt (§6).

**Did a choice ever feel arbitrary?** Yes, repeatedly. *New Workout Regimen* — "Risky, but it could
pay off" — is a pure coin flip with no basis to pick on, and it runs every offseason. *Redshirt?* and
*Saturday Night, Everybody Watching* are the same. The worst is the **Transfer Portal**, where the
only information that actually matters (the OVR delta) is hidden behind the click, so the choice is
arbitrary the first time and mechanical every time after.

**Invisible that should be visible:** draft stock / projected round (the whole game is about it and it
is never shown until the final screen); remaining eligibility; captaincy; whether "the two-deep is
thin" means anything at all.

**Prominent that did not deserve it: *Fall Camp Battle*.** A single-option card with no agency, no
choice, and frequently a forced −1, that ran **every season of every career — eight times**, four of
them with the identical "You press, and pressing looks exactly like panicking on film." It is the
first thing I see after the portal every single year and it is pure friction.

**What made me want to play again:** the position cards (kicker above all), the Moments reel the
first two times, and — most of all — **"Undrafted / No team called."** Proof of stakes was worth more
than anything else in the build.

**What made me want to stop:** the offseason block, and the discovery that the prose does not predict
the result.

**By career 4, what was I skimming?** Every card body. Every outcome sentence. The entire offseason
block. And the **"ON THE FIELD / IN THE LOCKER ROOM" status lines**, which I stopped looking at
altogether after career 2 because they changed without explanation, contradicted the stat line beside
them, and never told me anything I could act on. I was reading: **the two option buttons, the OVR
number, and the delta.** Nothing else.

## 5. One line — the single thing most in the way of a fifth career

**The offseason.** Redshirt? → Declare? → Transfer Portal → Fall Camp Battle → New Workout Regimen,
in that order, in identical words, every year, at every position — it is the first thing I hit each
season and it is the thing that teaches me not to read.

---

## 6. If you fix three things, fix these

1. **Break the offseason's fixed order** — and give *Fall Camp Battle* either a choice or the axe.
   This is the whole ballgame for the "play a fifth career" metric.
2. **Make the prose and the number agree.** Six exact instances in §8. Until a bad sentence means a
   bad number, reading the sentences is irrational, and no amount of new content will be read.
3. **Put a draft projection on the Declare card, and stop rendering "Undrafted" in celebration gold.**
   One turns a trap back into a gamble; the other stops the game congratulating a player it just
   failed.
