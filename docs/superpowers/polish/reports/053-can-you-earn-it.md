### Career 1 — answers

**A.1 Did the game ever tell you that you had gained something with the program?** Yes, twice, both
the same six words: **"Earned you credit in the locker room."** — D4 (coaching up the freshman
behind me) and D8 (holding the room together when the staff left). That is it, in 35 decisions.

**A.2 How many times did it comment on where I stood?** Four: two "Earned you credit in the locker
room" and two "Cost you in the locker room." **Too rarely** — and worse, unevenly: the two costs
both came off cards where I had not done anything socially wrong.

**A.3 Did you ever choose an option specifically to earn it?** Yes — **D21 "Your Team Now"**, where
the option is literally called **"Play it for the room."** Outcome: *"You play inside the call all
day and the young guys copy it."* **No number, no standing line, nothing.** The game did not notice.

**A.4 Built something, or avoided losing it?** *(answered before looking at anything else)*
**Neither, and that is worse than either.** I did not build anything — the ending screen put me on
the same rung I started on. But I did not feel I was defending anything either, because the readout
never moved, so there was never a moment where I felt something was at risk. I played four years of
deliberate generosity into a field that returned the same seven words on every screen. **The honest
sentence is: I could not tell the game was counting.**

**B.1 Proportionate?** No, inverted. The two biggest teammate decisions in the career — refusing
three transfers, and "Play it for the room" — drew **no comment at all**. Meanwhile "you are a step
late all afternoon" (a *football* sentence, D13) and "you do exactly what you did last summer" (a
*compliment*, D28) both drew **"Cost you in the locker room."** The comments landed on the wrong
cards.

**B.3 Wallpaper?** The "IN THE LOCKER ROOM / the staff trusts you" strip became wallpaper **by about
decision 12**, because it had been identical since before I had made a single choice. The in-card
"Earned you credit" lines did not become wallpaper — there were only four of them — but by D21 I had
stopped expecting them.

**C. Did being a good teammate cost me anything I could see?** Honestly, **no** — I went #2 overall.
The one card where generosity plausibly cost football was **D4 "Up Forty at Halftime"** (coach up the
freshman vs lobby for one more series); the game gave credit and no OVR, and I never felt behind.
So the tax does not exist. But neither does the reward.

**D. Ending screen**
1. **Loudest thing: "Round 1, Pick 2"** in huge gold type, with "#2 overall" under it. Nothing else
   is close. The locker-room ladder is small grey text below the rating.
2. Did it agree with the career? The draft slot, yes. **The ladder surprised me, downward** — I

3. **Did it tell me anything I did not already know?** Yes, exactly one thing, and it is the ladder:

4. **What made me want to run it back:** that rung. I want to know what it takes to get to *"they
   would run through a wall for you"*, and after four years of trying I have no idea.

because I think the difference is the whole point of the question. I am not sitting here wanting
another four years of the rivalry-week ankle. What I want is to settle one thing.

**What I am going to try to do differently:** get to **"they would run through a wall for you."**
Career 1 told me that rung exists and that four years of taking every generous option does not
reach it. Career 2 showed me the game *does* have a loud, boxed, unmistakable announcement when the
rung changes — it fired twice, both downward, one of them on decision 2. So my specific plan is:
take every locker-room-flavoured option *and* deliberately dodge the two cards that quietly cost me
standing in career 1 ("The First One" / "No Longer New" — the "Take care of business" branch — and
"Stay on campus and train"), and see whether the up-box exists at all.

**What on the ending screen put that idea in my head:** exactly one element — the five-rung ladder
with **"they would run through a wall for you"** sitting above my marker, in both careers. Nothing
else on either ending screen made me want to replay. The draft slot did not; the OVR curve did not;
the moments reel made me smile but not reload.

So: yes, and I am going to go chase the top rung.

## Career 3 — OKAFOR #7, Offensive line, UTSA (self-assigned: chase the top rung)

I played this one with one goal: reach **"they would run through a wall for you."** I took the
generous branch of every card that had one, and I deliberately avoided the two cards that had
quietly cost me standing in career 1.

What I did, in order: went home for the summer instead of the branch that punished me; "Let the game
come to you" instead of "Take care of business"; **coached up the freshman** (credit #1); played
through rivalry week for the team; **sat so the backup could play** (twice); **was first on my feet
when a teammate took my award** (credit #2); stayed at UTSA all four years and turned down four
transfer offers including two "bigger role" ones; waited my turn in the training-table line;
**held the room together** through two coaching changes (credits #3 and #4); played it for the room;
never mentioned the watch list; **had compliance read the sportsbook NIL deal and passed on
$40,000**; treated bowl prep like a second spring ball.

**Result: Round 4, Pick 11 (#107 overall). OVR 79. And in the locker room —**

```
  they would run through a wall for you

  just a guy in the building
  a problem they are managing
  the staff has stopped defending you
```

**Exactly the rung I started on. For the second time.** Four explicit "Earned you credit in the
locker room" messages, a career of nothing but the generous branch, and the readout that career 2
proved *can* move — loudly, with a box, on decision 2 — did not move once.

**Did the game let me chase it? Yes.** The generous options were always there and always clearly
labelled. **Did it notice I was trying? No.** Not once did anything on screen acknowledge a *pattern*
— that this player had done the teammate thing nine times running. Each card was scored alone and
forgotten.

### What career 3 added that the first two did not

- **The A/B on The Team Banquet is damning.** Generous branch ("Be the first one on your feet"):
  *"Nobody forgets who did that, least of all him."* + "Earned you credit" + **no change to overall**.
  Selfish branch, from career 2 ("Let it show"): *"...they like it."* + **+1 OVR** + no penalty.
  **The mean option pays a point and the kind option pays a token that never adds up to anything.**

  "Cut to the front" in career 2 cost standing. **Asymmetric by design or not, that is how it reads.**

## Overall answers

### The one question: is standing something you can earn, or only something you can lose?

**Only something you can lose.** Across three careers and ~100 decisions:
- **Upward rung movements: zero.** Two careers played specifically to earn it, six "Earned you
  credit in the locker room" messages between them, both finished on the starting rung.
- **Downward rung movements: two**, both in the one career where I misbehaved, the first of them on
  **decision 2**, each with a large, clear, well-designed callout box naming the rung I left.

The display is not the problem. The *asymmetry* is: the same widget that fires within ninety seconds
of a bad choice will not fire after four years of good ones.

### Claims

1. **Arrives only when something real happened, proportionate — PARTIAL / mostly REFUTED.** It is
   not constant, and it has not gone quiet in the down direction. But it is **not proportionate**:
   in career 1 the two largest teammate decisions in the career drew nothing while a *compliment*
   and a case of first-game nerves each drew "Cost you in the locker room"; in career 2 a card whose
   own prose says the press "move on by Monday" dropped me a whole rung. **In the up direction it
   has effectively gone quiet: six credits, zero visible effect, ever.**
2. **Standing is something you can earn, not only lose — REFUTED.** Two careers of maximal
   generosity, both ended on the starting rung. See above.
3. **Being a good teammate is not a tax on your football career — CONFIRMED.** Career 1 went #2
   overall; career 3 went round 4 at a position and program that explain it without reference to
   behaviour. Nothing I gave away visibly cost me a draft slot. **There is no tax. There is also no
   return.**
4. **The teammate cards read as the game working, not as paying you to do less — REFUTED, but not
   in the direction you feared.** They do not read as being paid to do less; "Sit and let the backup
   play" explicitly *costs* you grief and gives you nothing. They read as **the game not reacting at
   all.** The one that does react — The Team Banquet — gives the kind branch a token and the mean
   branch a point. My honest sentence: *these cards feel like they were written to be good and
   wired to be inert.*
5. **The start of each season is a decision, not a toll — REFUTED.** Every season opened with
   Redshirt? and/or Transfer Portal. Declining the redshirt produced **no outcome screen at all**,
   three times. Choosing to stay produced nothing twice and a bookkeeping number twice. **That is
   the definition of a toll: a screen I clicked through to continue.**
6. **The ledger agrees with the season — CONFIRMED.** No mismatched headings, no season skipped, no
   "injured" where I was suspended. Year rows, records, class years and snap counts all lined up
   across three careers. The "OVR BEFORE THE OFFSEASON" heading explains the one apparent
   discrepancy (ledger 87 vs header 89).
7. **The card's number agrees with its words — REFUTED, five instances.** Listed in the career 2
   section; the worst are career 1 D28 (*"in August it shows that you did"* + "Cost you" + −1 OVR)
   and career 2 D34 (sulking rewarded with +1 and a promise about a season a senior cannot play).
   The single-option cards whose button asserts success and whose outcome is failure ("Win the job",
   "Answer it on the practice field", "Trust the rebuild") happened **three times across three
   careers** and are the cheapest to fix.
8. **You always know where you are — CONFIRMED.** Season, class year, school, phase, snaps and
   rating were always on screen and never disagreed with each other. This is solid.
9. **Anything hard to read, cut off, overflowing or colliding — NONE.** Three careers at 500px, no
   visual defects.

**The cheap thing:** I saw **0 snaps** with a card claiming *"You play inside the call all day"*
(career 3, season 3 non-conference), but **no fake stat line** — the stats were simply absent. No
large fake line anywhere. One line, as instructed.

### The brief's closing questions

- **Bored / lost:** around **decision 22** in career 1, when Rivalry Week's barking ankle arrived for
  the third straight season with identical text. By career 3 I had seen it ten times.
- **Arbitrary choices:** "Take care of business" vs "Let the game come to you" / "Trust the work you
  put in" / "Take nothing that is not there" — the same non-choice reskinned three times, and one
  branch silently costs standing.
- **Invisible that should have been visible:** **the five-rung ladder.** It is the spine of the
  thing and it is only ever shown after the career is over. Show me the rung above me on decision 1
  and career 1 becomes a game.
- **Prominent that did not deserve it:** the draft slot on the ending screen. It is four times the
  size of everything else and it is the number I had the least control over.
- **Wanted to play again:** the rung above my marker. **Wanted to stop:** finding out, twice, that
  it does not move.

> **One line — the single thing most in the way of me playing one more career:**
> **I now believe the top rung is unreachable, so the only lever the game gives me for the thing it
> says it is about is the one that goes down.**

### Would I play a fourth?

**No.** Not because I am bored — the writing is genuinely good and I enjoyed the mercenary. Because
I set myself a goal, played a whole career at it with full information about how the system
announces itself, and got the same six-word label I started with. There is nothing left for me to
try. That puts me on the same curve as most of the seven before me: *yes → yes → no.*
