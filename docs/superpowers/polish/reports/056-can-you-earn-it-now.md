## THE THIRD CAREER — my answer, written before I did anything else

> **Do I want to play again right now — yes or no?**

**Yes — but a reluctant, narrow yes, and I want to be precise about what kind.** I do not want to play another four-year story. I want to **check one thing**, and the thing is on the ending screen in both careers:

> **they would run through a wall for you**

That rung exists. In career 1 I played thirty-four decisions of deliberate, costly good-teammate football — taught the walk-on, took the blocks, gave my touches away, stayed twice, played hurt — and finished on the **same rung I was handed on the character-creation screen**. In career 2 I ducked and ghosted and got moved **down twice, loudly, with the old rung named out loud**. So I now know the down channel works and I have no evidence the up channel exists at all.

**What I am going to try to do differently:** play a third career aimed squarely at the top rung — never sit, never skip, take every teammate option, and **watch the `IN THE LOCKER ROOM` line after every single card** instead of watching my OVR. **What put the idea in my head:** the ending-screen ladder, and specifically the fact that it is the **only** place in the game that ever shows you there are five rungs. That ladder is doing recruitment for a replay it never mentions while you are playing.

**Honest caveat:** this is a *tester's* yes, not a *player's* yes. If I were not going to write it down, the thing that would actually have made me start career 3 is the school chain across the top of career 2's screen — `James Madison → Toledo → North Texas → Baylor` — because it made me want to see what a stay-at-one-school-and-win version of that line looks like. That is a smaller pull than the ladder, but it is the more genuine one.

## THE ANSWER, in my own words

> **Is standing with the program something you can earn, or only something you can lose?**

**It is now something you can be *thanked* for and still only *lose*.**

Since the last playtester, a genuine upward signal has been built: **"Earned you credit in the locker room."**, identical gold, identical weight, identical position to **"Cost you in the locker room."** That is a real fix and it refutes the flat "only something you can lose" as a statement about the *text*. ## Answers to the three bets — final

1. **"The standing system now moves in both directions and is worth watching."**
   **PARTIAL, leaning refuted.** The *comment* moves both ways; the *state* does not. And here is the plain answer the brief asked for: **in career 1 it read as inert and I stopped tracking it around decision 12.** In career 3 I was watching it deliberately and it still never moved. **It is worth reading — it is not yet worth watching**, because the thing you watch (the rung) never responds. One number you could change: make N credit lines promote a rung the way N cost lines demote one; four was not enough, and I do not believe any reachable number currently is.
2. **"Intention before outcome."** **Neutral, registered, fine — not hedging, not telegraphing.** The one real defect is that on no-choice cards the same slot says **"The plan: Win the job"** about a plan I was never offered, and then sometimes blames me for it (−1 OVR, "pressing looks exactly like panicking on film").
3. **"Cards about helping a teammate read as the right thing being recognised."**
   **Split, and the split is the finding. It is not "payment for passivity" anywhere** — no teammate card ever felt like being paid to do less. But it is **inconsistent**: *Up Forty at Halftime* (coach the freshman) **is** recognition. *The Two-Deep* (teach the walk-on), *Playing Through the Hand* (play in a cast), *Feature Back Workload* (give your touches away), *Asked to Block*, *Your Team Now*, *Cutting the Line* — **all inert**, and one of them says its own quiet part out loud: *"Nobody notices, which is fine."*
