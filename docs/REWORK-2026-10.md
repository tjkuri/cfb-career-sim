# The fun rework (October 2026)

The owner played the polished build and called it repetitive and not fun. This
rework replaces the polish-phase direction in `docs/superpowers/polish/`. Those
documents are history now. Where they conflict with this file, this file wins.

## What the reference game actually does

From its engine (`reference/soccer-original/`):

- About 15 decisions in a whole career. Each one plays out two seasons, and
  most of them are "which club?". Only 3–4 random events happen per career.
- Every gamble shows its stakes on the button, e.g. "70% +3 OVR / 30% −2 OVR"
  vs "No changes".
- Effects are big and mechanical: OVR, starter vs bench, title odds ×2 or ×0.5.
- Finals are decided by a penalty-kick guess.
- Trophies land as they happen, and the trophy case is always on screen.

## What this game does now

**Season loop.** Each season has an offseason gamble, a mid-season gamble,
sometimes a bowl-week card, and then the big game. The transfer portal opens
every offseason, and a redshirt is offered when eligible. That works out to
about 21 decisions per career (it was 40). A random card never repeats within
a career (`GameState.firedEventIds`). The season-opener, fall-camp and
bowl-week milestones are gone; Senior Day is the only scripted beat.

**Odds are visible.** Every option shows its probability as a bar plus chips
for what each branch does (`ui/session/game-view.ts` `effectChips`). A result
says "It pays off" or "It backfires" in green or red.

**Depth chart** (`engine/role.ts`). Your role is Starter, Rotation or Backup,
based on OVR vs the program's bar (FCS 58, G5 65, P4 72, Blueblood 78). Role
scales snaps, development, and how much you move the team's record. National
awards go to starters only. This makes Signing Day and the transfer portal
real trades: play now somewhere small, or sit somewhere big.

**The big game** (`engine/big-game.ts`, `content/big-game.ts`,
`ui/screens/BigGame.tsx`). Each season ends on the biggest game the team
reached: national title, conference title, bowl, or rivalry. Each play
starts from a generated spot (score, clock, down, distance, field position),
drawn on a field with the line of scrimmage and the line to gain, and the
final score follows from it (down 4 plus a touchdown is a 3-point win).

The player gets two clues and calls one of three plays:
- **Film:** their favourite call here and how often they run it. The
  percentage changes every game (35–90%).
- **Pre-snap look:** what they show, which is honest 60% of the time.

**Situation playbooks.** Each position has two packages, each with its own
three calls, three looks and odds (`PLAYBOOKS` in `content/big-game.ts`), and
consecutive big games switch packages 75% of the time:

| position | package 1 | package 2 |
|---|---|---|
| QB | red zone | last gasp (four verticals from deep) |
| RB/WR | red zone | last gasp, as the Z receiver |
| OL | goal line | max protect, at left guard |
| DL | stop | goal-line stand, at end |
| LB/DB | cover | Hail Mary, at corner |
| K | field goal (wind) | chip shot vs. block-team looks |

`makeSpot` places the spot to match the package, and the scorebug shows the
package's name. Every snap is the last one: 4th down, or 3rd down only with
5 seconds or less left.

The calls and defenses use real football terms and real matchups: the
corner route (smash) beats Cover 2, four verticals beats Cover 3, a speed
rush loses to the draw, a steep kick beats the leaper. In the cover
package the receiver's split is the tell: tight to the formation means an
out, wide by the sideline means a slant. Each call's result lines are
written to fit any outcome, since one line covers every defense.

When the clues conflict, the right read depends on the number: past about
60%, trust the film; under it, trust the look. Measured: random calling wins
~55%, either clue alone ~65%, reading both 59–71% depending on the package. After the snap, the ball
animates to where the play ended, and the card grades the call as strong,
even or weak against what they actually ran.

Win and you get the trophy, +1 OVR, +3 Rep and a moment. A backup gets
thrown in 60% of the time; a player who is hurt or redshirting watches.

**The telestrator** (`ui/telestrator/`). The big game takes over the screen
(the player card and ledger collapse to a strip) and draws the snap from a
broadcast skycam: a real perspective projection, X's and O's, the defense in
the look it showed. When they disguise, you watch them shift at the snap.
Your play is inked in telestrator yellow, your own path dashed in gold when
the ball is in someone else's hands, and the defender who made the stop in
red. There is no caption on the field; the result card says what happened.
Each package has its own staging in `choreo.ts`, and deep shots pull the
camera back to frame the end zone. `choreo.test.ts` checks that the picture
never contradicts the result (a touchdown ends in the end zone, a stop is
short of the line to gain) across every call, look and spot.

Phones get a tighter camera that follows your player. Picked over a
three.js version after side-by-side mocks: it reads better and costs nothing
to download.

The play is modelled as a drive state (`Spot`), so a multi-snap drive (the
planned next step) is the same shape with more snaps.

**Stakes flavour.** Each big-game tier has its own intro lines, rotated so a
repeat trip never reads the same, and its own colour: rivalry red, bowl
bronze, conference silver, national title gold. Every school has a fixed
rival and an invented trophy ("Battle for the Copper Kettle"). Title wins
fire confetti and lift the trophy in.

**Trophy art** (`ui/screens/Trophy.tsx`). Original SVG trophies, one per
honour, used in the trophy case, the recap, the big-game win and the
draft-night cabinet. None of them copies a real trophy.

**Rep and the captain's C** (`engine/role.ts`). Rep 85 or more makes you
captain for as long as it lasts. A captain wins depth-chart ties (counts as
+3 OVR against the starter bar) and gets +5% on the big game's last snap.
The C shows on the player card, the broadcast strip and the ledger, and an
option that would gain or lose it says so ("Earn the C" / "Lose the C").
Rep costs on cards were doubled and Rep gains multiplied by 2.5 so the meter
moves, and a big-game win gives +2 Rep (was +3). Measured over 1,500
careers: random play is captain at some point in 12% of careers, dips to the
temptation band (55) in 41% and the bust band (35) in 10%; always taking the
clean option makes captain in 58%.

**Progression: growth is earned.** Each offseason adds +1 after the
freshman year only, then +3 as a starter, +2 in the rotation and +0 on the
bench, plus the level you practice against: Group of 5 +1, Power Four +2,
Blueblood +3, plus ±1. Sitting at a big program grows you about as fast as
starting at a small one, which keeps Signing Day a real choice (with smart
play, signing as a starter, rotation or backup all reach round 1 about 30%
of the time). School cards show the estimate ("About +4 OVR from a season
there").

**Risk has teeth.** Every gamble's OVR downside was doubled, and any gamble
that fell below its alternatives on average got a bigger upside, so a
gamble costs about the same on average but swings harder. Blind gambling
has a higher ceiling and a lower floor than playing safe; choosing good
gambles is best. Injuries leave a lasting mark (about a third of the hit,
a torn ACL keeps −5), shown on the chip ("Torn ACL −14, −5 for good").
The redshirt costs 8 draft stock on draft night (a fifth-year senior), so
it pays when you are on the bench and not when you start.
`src/sim/content-audit.ts` values injuries by their lasting cost and lists
the options still dominated; the ones left trade Rep, on purpose.

**Class labels.** A redshirt keeps your class year, so labels go FR, RS, RS-SO
and so on, and no career shows SR twice.

**The ladder** (`engine/transfer.ts` `programCalls`). Bigger programs have
to be earned. A school calls in the portal only when your stock clears its
tier's bar (`TIER_CALL`: G5 28, P4 48, Blueblood 76), and starters get a
15-point discount. Signing Day's Blueblood reach offer shows up 15% of the
time. Each school card lists what the tier gives you (`TIER_PERK`) and the
role you'd have there. Random play reaches a Blueblood in about 16% of
careers.

**Draft.** Stock comes from OVR, production, honors (counted every season they
are won), the best tier you played at, and Rep. In the career it shows as a
big-board rank, which climbs from #500s toward #1. Draft night runs a pick
ticker down to your name, then opens the trophy cabinet and a scouting
report: two things scouts liked, two concerns, and how many stock points
short of the next round you were (`scouting()` in `ui/session/summary.ts`).

**Balance** (`npm run balance`, 1,500 careers per policy):

| policy | Rd 1 | UDFA | Heisman per career |
|---|---|---|---|
| random | ~10% | ~19% | ~1% |
| smart (best EV, counter the scout) | ~33% | ~13% | ~8% |
| always the safe option | 0% | ~12% | 0% |

Taking risks is how you get drafted high, and it can also sink you. Playing
it safe every time gets you a steady, late-round career.

## Removed

The program-standing ladder (character shown as five phrases), the "hue may
never track valence" rule, the rule against showing probabilities, the old
view-model, ledger and bands layers, and the measurement probes that encoded
those decisions (`src/sim/*-probe.ts`, `policies.ts`, `balance.ts`).

## Content rules

`npx tsx src/sim/content-audit.ts` lists the most-seen cards, options that are
dominated (better on average *and* in the worst case), and third-person
moments. Position cards weigh 3x in the draw (`POSITION_CARD_BOOST`). A risky
option must show a visibly bigger ceiling than the safe one. Off-field
temptations trade Rep for something on the field; the low-Rep spiral cards
keep a trap option and a redemption that costs OVR.

## Known next steps

- More offseason position cards: each position has only ~2, so the offseason
  slot still leans on the shared off-field pool.
- Cards that fire in the wrong context (an award watch list during a redshirt
  season).
- A bigger pool of plays per package, so a long career doesn't see the same
  three calls twice.
- **Drive mode (B):** big games become short drives whose length scales with
  the stakes (rivalry 1 snap → national title 3–4), with defences adjusting
  after you beat them, goal-line stands on defence, and a kicker whose kick
  length comes from where the drive stalls.
- Re-check the All-Conference stat thresholds against the new snap model.
