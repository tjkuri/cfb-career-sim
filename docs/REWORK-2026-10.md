# The fun rework (October 2026)

The owner played the polished build and called it repetitive and not fun. This
rework replaces the polish-phase direction in `docs/superpowers/polish/`. Those
documents are history now. Where they conflict with this file, this file wins.

## What the reference game actually does

From its engine (`reference/soccer-original/`):

- About 15 decisions in a whole career. Each one plays out two seasons, and
  most of them are "which club?". Only 3–4 random events happen per career.
- Every gamble shows its stakes on the button, e.g. "70% +3 OVR / 30% −2 OVR"
  vs "No changes".
- Effects are big and mechanical: OVR, starter vs bench, title odds ×2 or ×0.5.
- Finals are decided by a penalty-kick guess.
- Trophies land as they happen, and the trophy case is always on screen.

## What this game does now

**Season loop.** Each season has an offseason gamble, a mid-season gamble,
sometimes a bowl-week card, and then the big game. The transfer portal opens
every offseason, and a redshirt is offered when eligible. That works out to
about 21 decisions per career (it was 40). A random card never repeats within
a career (`GameState.firedEventIds`). The season-opener, fall-camp and
bowl-week milestones are gone; Senior Day is the only scripted beat.

**Odds are visible.** Every option shows its probability as a bar plus chips
for what each branch does (`ui/session/game-view.ts` `effectChips`). A result
says "It pays off" or "It backfires" in green or red.

**Depth chart** (`engine/role.ts`). Your role is Starter, Rotation or Backup,
based on OVR vs the program's bar (FCS 58, G5 65, P4 72, Blueblood 78). Role
scales snaps, development, and how much you move the team's record. National
awards go to starters only. This makes Signing Day and the transfer portal
real trades: play now somewhere small, or sit somewhere big.

**The big game** (`engine/big-game.ts`, `content/big-game.ts`,
`ui/screens/BigGame.tsx`). Each season ends on the biggest game the team
reached: national title, conference title, bowl, or rivalry. Each play
starts from a generated spot (score, clock, down, distance, field position),
drawn on a field with the line of scrimmage and the line to gain, and the
final score follows from it (down 4 plus a touchdown is a 3-point win).

The player gets two clues and calls one of three plays:
- **Film:** their favourite call here and how often they run it. The
  percentage changes every game (35–90%).
- **Pre-snap look:** what they show, which is honest 60% of the time.

When the clues conflict, the right read depends on the number: past about
60%, trust the film; under it, trust the look. Measured: random calling wins
~55%, either clue alone ~65%, reading both ~68%. After the snap, the ball
animates to where the play ended, and the card grades the call as strong,
even or weak against what they actually ran.

Win and you get the trophy, +1 OVR, +3 Rep and a moment. A backup gets
thrown in 60% of the time; a player who is hurt or redshirting watches.

The play is modelled as a drive state (`Spot`), so a multi-snap drive (the
planned next step) is the same shape with more snaps.

**Stakes flavour.** Each big-game tier has its own intro lines, rotated so a
repeat trip never reads the same, and its own colour: rivalry red, bowl
bronze, conference silver, national title gold. Every school has a fixed
rival and an invented trophy ("Battle for the Copper Kettle"). Title wins
fire confetti and lift the trophy in.

**Trophy art** (`ui/screens/Trophy.tsx`). Original SVG trophies, one per
honour, used in the trophy case, the recap, the big-game win and the
draft-night cabinet. None of them copies a real trophy.

**Progression.** Each offseason grows OVR by a class-year base, plus a role
bonus, plus ±1. A redshirt keeps your class year, so labels go FR, RS, RS-SO
and so on, and no career shows SR twice.

**Draft.** Stock comes from OVR, production, honors (counted every season they
are won), the best tier you played at, and Rep. In the career it shows as a
big-board rank, which climbs from #500s toward #1.

**Balance** (`npm run balance`, 1,200 careers per policy):

| policy | Rd 1 | UDFA | Heisman per career |
|---|---|---|---|
| random | ~10% | ~8% | ~1% |
| smart (best EV, counter the scout) | ~43% | ~1% | ~5% |
| always the safe option | 0% | ~27% | 0% |

Taking risks is how you get drafted. Playing it safe every time gets you a
mediocre career.

## Removed

The program-standing ladder (character shown as five phrases), the "hue may
never track valence" rule, the rule against showing probabilities, the old
view-model, ledger and bands layers, and the measurement probes that encoded
those decisions (`src/sim/*-probe.ts`, `policies.ts`, `balance.ts`).

## Content rules

`npx tsx src/sim/content-audit.ts` lists the most-seen cards, options that are
dominated (better on average *and* in the worst case), and third-person
moments. Position cards weigh 3x in the draw (`POSITION_CARD_BOOST`). A risky
option must show a visibly bigger ceiling than the safe one. Off-field
temptations trade Rep for something on the field; the low-Rep spiral cards
keep a trap option and a redemption that costs OVR.

## Known next steps

- More offseason position cards: each position has only ~2, so the offseason
  slot still leans on the shared off-field pool.
- Cards that fire in the wrong context (an award watch list during a redshirt
  season).
- **Drive mode (B):** big games become short drives whose length scales with
  the stakes (rivalry 1 snap → national title 3–4), with defences adjusting
  after you beat them, goal-line stands on defence, and a kicker whose kick
  length comes from where the drive stalls.
- Re-check the All-Conference stat thresholds against the new snap model.
